## A. Research brief

1. **Clients are coming in less often, and new clients are harder to find.** Several independent booking-platform datasets agree: new-guest visits fell across salon segments in 2025, barbershops worst (Zenoti, -17%); waxing studios report new-guest acquisition down and flat same-store revenue (Zenoti); salons report clients stretching time between visits (Kline via Beauty Independent); the smallest salons (1 to 2 stylists) were the only cohort clearly down in Q1 2026 while average ticket rose (Salon Today). Consumer surveys conflict on how much clients are cutting back (Booksy: 34% cut back; Boulevard: 73% keep their visits). Prices for personal care services rose about 37.6% from Aug 2019 to Aug 2026 (HomeWise Review citing BLS; not confirmed firsthand). **Act now:** this is the most useful thing to write about for small studios this winter.
2. **The Korean lash lift is the clearest new consumer demand.** US searches went from about 15k a month (Nov 2025) to a 74k peak (Jun 2026) and sit near 50k (Oct 2026). Fresha reported the same direction in its own press data. Questions with real volume: "what is a Korean lash lift" and "Korean lash lift vs regular lash lift" (about 2.9k a month each, rising). **Act now:** drafted.
3. **Builder gel keeps growing in nails** (74k to 110k a month over the year), and "builder gel vs gel x" is a rising comparison (8.1k average, 10.6k recent). Russian manicure searches are up too, with a state-by-state legality debate built on weak sources. Press-on searches fell.
4. **State "all-in pricing" laws are spreading** (California, Colorado, Connecticut from July 2026, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Oregon, Virginia, per law-firm reviews). No source applies them to salons specifically, but a studio that adds a mandatory booking or service fee on top of the listed price is the kind of practice they target. Worth one careful paragraph in our deposit and fee articles, never legal advice.
5. **Smaller and seasonal signals:** warm brunettes, soft reds and gloss services for winter color (gloss searches up); soft, natural brow lamination; sugaring "near me" up about half; fine-line tattoo steady, micro-realism down; curated ear stacks small but rising; the low taper fade and buzz cut falling from very high levels.

### E2. Client retention guide, new section (update, pending approval)

Where: `content/blog/client-retention-strategies.md`, a new `##` section before "Catch clients before they fully lapse." Nothing else changes, and the publish date stays as it is (the generator records the modified date by itself).

What changed and why: the guide assumes clients keep their rhythm and only lapse at the end. This cycle's strongest evidence is that regulars are stretching the gap, which needs different advice: book the interval they actually keep, and offer a shorter service in between, instead of discounting.

Proposed text:

> ## When clients start coming in less often
>
> Many studios are seeing the same pattern: regulars still come, but further apart, and fewer new clients find their way in. When the gap between visits stretches, the answer is in how you rebook, not in a discount.
>
> **Rebook at the interval they actually keep.** If a color client used to come every six weeks and now comes every nine, offer nine. A booked visit at nine weeks beats an unbooked one at six.
>
> **Offer something shorter in between.** A gloss, a fringe trim, a brow tint, a polish change. A short visit keeps you in their routine in a month when a full service is not in the budget. Put each one on your menu as a real service with its own time and price, so clients can book it without asking.
>
> **Keep the price visible before they ask.** Clients watching their money book more readily when the full price is on the page and nothing is added at the end.
>
> **Do not cut the price of the main service to close the gap.** It teaches clients to wait for the next cut.
>
> :::copy Rebooking line for a client who is stretching visits
> It's been about [weeks] weeks since your last visit, which seems to suit you. Want me to book you in at [weeks] weeks again? And if you want a quick refresh before then, a [short service] takes [time].
> :::

Sources: [Zenoti](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/barbershop/barbershop-trends-2026), [Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/fewer-clients-stronger-revenue-why-your-pricing-power-is-your-biggest-q1-win), [Beauty Independent / Kline](https://www.beautyindependent.com/kline-u-s-salon-revenue-drops-clients-stretch-time-between-visits/), [Meevo playbook](https://www.meevo.com/blog/?p=12905).
