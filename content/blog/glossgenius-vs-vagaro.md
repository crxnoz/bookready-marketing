The choice between GlossGenius and Vagaro is mostly a question about your business, not about software. One is built to stay simple for a solo pro or a small team. The other is built to run a salon with staff, retail, classes and memberships.

:::answer
If you work alone or with one or two other people and you want one subscription, one bill and a tidy booking page you set up once, GlossGenius is the easier fit. If you run a multi-chair salon with retail, classes, memberships or payroll, Vagaro goes deeper and the extra settings earn their keep. Both prefer you to process card payments through them, and that, not the monthly fee, is what makes either one hard to leave.
:::

## They are built for different sizes of business

GlossGenius is beauty-native and opinionated. A booking page, card processing and a card reader are bundled into the subscription, so there is one bill and one support line, and the words in the app are the words you use: fills, add-ons, deposits, tips, color notes. The flip side of opinionated is less room to configure: when your way of working does not match what the app expects, there is often no setting for it.

Vagaro is the opposite trade: more staff, payroll, inventory, memberships, packages, classes, a marketplace listing, hardware, and more settings than most solo pros will ever open. Six chairs, a product wall and a Saturday class is what that depth is for. It is also the learning curve. The owners who love Vagaro usually have a front desk that learned it properly. The ones who resent it are one person inside software built for twelve chairs.

## The cost does not grow the same way

GlossGenius leans toward a flatter shape: a subscription, with the website and the reader inside it. Vagaro grows with you, because you pay as you add staff calendars and as you switch on add-ons for payroll or marketing, so two Vagaro salons can pay very different amounts.

Both change their prices and what each tier includes, so do not trust a figure from an article, including this one. Price the tier you would be on from each company's own page, add the processing percentage, and use the staff count you will need twelve months from now rather than today's.

## Both want your card payments, and that is the real lock-in

With both, the booking software and the card processing are one relationship. You do not shop a rate, and your payout timing, holds and chargebacks sit with the company that runs your calendar.

So your true cost is the subscription plus a percentage of everything you take, which grows with your revenue whatever the plan says. Your clients' saved cards are tokens held by that processor and do not transfer. Leaving means changing your software and your merchant account the same day.

> The subscription is the number you compare. The processing is the number you actually pay.

## The marketplace wins you clients, then charges you for regulars

Vagaro has a consumer-facing marketplace where clients search for salons near them and book. GlossGenius does not lean on one. That is a genuine difference, not a gimmick.

When your book has gaps, a marketplace is one of the few sources of new clients that does not require you to post every day. A new esthetician in a new city can fill a Wednesday afternoon from it. Once you are full of regulars, the same listing is something you keep paying for: regulars who would have booked you directly go through it instead, and you sit in the results beside the salon two blocks away.

Check it this week: sort last month's new clients by how they found you, and the listing either earned a real share of them or it did not.

## Five things that actually decide it

|  | GlossGenius | Vagaro |
|---|---|---|
| Built for | Beauty, solo pros and small teams | Any size, best for a multi-staff salon |
| How the cost grows | Flatter, subscription plus processing | With staff calendars and add-ons |
| Where clients find you | Your link, Instagram, Google | The same, plus Vagaro's marketplace |
| How configurable it is | Opinionated, fast, hard to bend | Deep settings, real learning curve |
| What leaves with you | Client contacts, from your own export | Same. Link, reviews, history stay behind |

## Who should pick which

A solo lash artist, nail tech, brow specialist or barber who wants one bill and a tidy booking page: GlossGenius.

A multi-chair salon or spa with retail, classes, memberships or payroll: Vagaro. The depth is the point, and it beats running three systems that each know half of a client's story. If you want the wider field instead, there is a longer list of [Vagaro alternatives](/blog/vagarocom-alternatives/).

If your main problem is that your booking page looks like everyone else's, neither is the answer. Both set the structure of that page for everyone on the platform: you get your logo, photos, colors and service list, not how the page is built or how it reads. Moving between them swaps one house style for another. What solves that is a site on your own domain, which is where [BKRDY](/templates/) sits.

## Keep your own export, whichever you choose

Your client contacts can come with you, reliably only if you keep your own export. Your booking link, your reviews, the page itself and saved cards do not. So export your client list every few months, on any platform, the way you back up photos.

:::copy Checklist to run before you switch booking platforms

1. Export your client list to a CSV, open it, and check the names, phone numbers and emails came through. Save it somewhere that is not the old platform.
2. Copy your service menu: every name, duration and price, add-ons included.
3. Write down every money setting as it stands: deposit amount, whether it is refundable, your cancellation window of 24 hours, your no-show fee, how far ahead your books open.
4. List every place the old link lives: Instagram bio, Google Business Profile, Facebook, your site, your text signature, your window sticker. Redirect it if you can, and keep the old account open one more billing cycle.
5. Tell your clients twice, a week apart, and change the link in your bio and on your Google profile the day you send the first message.
6. Move your future appointments across by hand and confirm each one.

:::

Work down that list before you cancel anything and the switch is boring, which is what you want. Coming off Vagaro, the step-by-step version is in the [migration guide](/guides/migrate-from-vagaro/).

## Questions

### Is GlossGenius better than Vagaro?

For a solo pro or a small team, usually yes: it is simpler and the cost shape is flatter. For a multi-chair salon with retail, classes, memberships or payroll, Vagaro fits better, because those things live inside it.

### Which one is cheaper, GlossGenius or Vagaro?

It depends on your staff count and your add-ons. GlossGenius tends toward a flatter subscription, Vagaro grows as you add calendars and modules. Both change their prices, so check each company's own page and add the processing percentage first.

### What happens to my reviews if I switch platforms?

They stay, because reviews left on a booking platform belong to that profile. Good reason to also collect on your Google Business Profile: ask every client rather than only the happy ones, never offer anything in exchange, never write or buy reviews, and never ask the rating question first so you can route unhappy people elsewhere. Those practices can get reviews removed.
