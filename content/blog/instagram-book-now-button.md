There are two ways to get a booking off your Instagram profile, and they look alike from the outside. One is an action button, the kind a professional account can add under the bio, labelled something like "Book now". The other is a plain link in your bio that points at your booking page.

They open different things. The button opens whatever booking service it is connected to, on that service's terms. The link opens exactly the page you chose. For most beauty pros, the link is the one to rely on.

:::answer
An action button on a professional Instagram profile opens a booking flow from one of the partner services Instagram works with, so it only helps if your booking software is one of them, and the page the client sees is theirs. A link in bio opens any page you choose, including your own booking page. If your booking page is your own, the link in bio is the one to use, and the button is optional at best.
:::

## What an action button actually is

A professional account (business or creator) can add action buttons to the profile. Instagram changes the exact menus and names often, so look in your profile editing settings for something like action buttons or contact options. Depending on your account and region, you will typically see choices for contacting you (call, email, directions) and for booking or reserving.

The booking button is the important one to understand. It is not a box where you paste any link you like. You pick a provider from a list of booking services that have an arrangement with Instagram, and you connect your account on that service. When a client taps the button, Instagram opens that provider's booking flow, sometimes inside Instagram itself.

That has three consequences:

- **It only works with services on the list.** If your booking software is not one of them, there is no way to point the button at your page.
- **The client sees the provider's flow, not your site.** Your name and services come through, but the look, the layout and anything else on the screen are set by that provider.
- **The list changes.** Partners are added and removed over time, so a button that works today is something to check rather than assume.

None of that makes the button bad. If your bookings already run through one of those services and you are happy there, the button is a convenient extra tap target, and it costs you little to switch it on.

## What a link in bio opens

The link in your bio opens whatever address you put there. Instagram usually opens it in its own built-in browser, so the client stays in the app, but the page they see is yours: your booking page, your photos, your policies, your Book button.

That is the difference in one line. The button opens their booking flow for you. The link opens your page.

For a beauty pro whose booking page is their own website, that matters for three reasons:

1. **The client sees what you built.** Your gallery and your policies are on the page they land on, not a stripped-down version.
2. **It works with any booking software.** You are not limited to whoever is on Instagram's partner list this year.
3. **It is the same link everywhere.** The same address in your bio, your Stories, your texts and on the card at your counter, so clients learn one place to book.

Adding the link is covered step by step in the guide to [adding your booking link to your Instagram bio](/guides/add-booking-link-to-instagram-bio/), including what to write above it. This article is only about which of the two to rely on.

## Why your own link is usually the one to use

The button is optional. The link is not. Every professional profile has a place for a link, and it is the one tap target you fully control.

There is a quieter reason, too. When your booking lives on someone else's flow, you depend on two arrangements you do not control: that provider's, and Instagram's arrangement with that provider. A plain link depends only on your page being up.

## The honest trade-off

A button can look more official. It sits in a row under the bio where people expect buttons, and some clients will tap a button sooner than a line of text. If your booking software is an Instagram partner and you like how its flow looks, use both: the button for the people who look for a button, and the link for everyone else.

What you give up with a button only is control. If the provider changes its flow, leaves the partner list, or puts things on the screen you did not choose, there is nothing you can do from your side. And if you ever move your bookings somewhere else, the button stops working the day you leave, while a link just gets a new address.

If you rely on marketplace bookings and most of your new clients find you inside that marketplace, the trade looks different again. That is a question about where clients come from, not about the button, and it is worth answering with your own numbers before you change anything.

## Check what a stranger sees, this week

You have probably never tapped your own profile the way a new client does. Do it once.

1. **Open your profile from a second account, or a friend's phone.** Not your own logged-in view, which can show you things a visitor does not see.
2. **Tap every button and the link in bio.** Note where each one lands, how many taps it takes to reach a booking time, and whether the page looks like you.
3. **Remove anything that leads somewhere stale.** An old booking service you no longer use, a link to a page that moved, a button connected to an account you closed.
4. **Make your booking link the first thing in the bio links,** if you have more than one.

## Questions

### What is the difference between Instagram's Book now button and a link in bio?

A Book now action button opens a booking flow from one of the services Instagram partners with, so the client sees that provider's flow. A link in bio opens any page you choose, including your own booking page.

### Can I point Instagram's Book now button at my own booking page?

Usually not. The booking button connects to a provider from Instagram's partner list rather than to any link you paste. If your booking software is not on that list, use the link in your bio instead.

### Should I use both the button and the link?

You can, if your booking software is an Instagram partner and you like its flow. The link in bio is the one to rely on either way, because it works with any booking page and you control where it goes.
