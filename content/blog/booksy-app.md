Booksy is a booking marketplace, and "the Booksy app" is really two apps. Clients download one to find a barber or stylist near them and book. Pros download the other, Booksy Biz, to run the calendar those bookings land in. The two are one system: what you set up in Biz is what a client sees in theirs.

That matters before you download anything, because the question is not whether the app is good. It is whether you want to be listed in a marketplace, and what that listing will cost you once you are busy.

## Two apps, one marketplace

The client app is free for clients. It shows businesses near them, their services and prices, their photos and reviews, and the open times. A client makes an account, books, and gets their reminders through it.

Booksy Biz is the business side, and it is where your subscription sits. You set your services, prices, hours and staff, and your calendar fills from three places: clients who found you by searching, clients who came straight to your profile from a link you shared, and appointments you add yourself.

The connection is the whole product. Your profile in the client app is your shop window, and Biz is the back room behind it.

## What Booksy Biz is for

On the pro side you get what you would expect from a booking tool:

- A calendar for you or your staff, with online booking from your profile.
- A client list with visit history and notes.
- Reminders sent to clients before their appointment.
- Card payments and deposit or no-show protection through Booksy's own processing.
- Your profile, photos and reviews inside the marketplace.
- Tools to promote your listing so it shows higher in search.

That last line is where the money conversation starts. The costs, add-ons and per-staff pricing are covered in [Booksy pricing](/blog/booksy-pricing/), so they are not repeated here. Prices and fee rules change, so check Booksy's own site for the deal you would get today.

## What the marketplace gives, and what it takes

What it gives is real. A client who has never heard of you types "fade" or "braids" into the app, and you come up. For a barber who has just moved cities, or a stylist with empty Tuesdays, that is one of the few sources of new clients that does not depend on posting every day.

What it takes is less visible. Your regulars save your Booksy profile as the way to book you, so you stay on it to keep them. Every time they open it, you are listed beside the shop down the street. Promotion inside the marketplace has carried a fee on new clients it brings you, so ask exactly how a client is counted as new before you switch it on.

> A marketplace is a lead source while you are building, and a toll road once you are full.

## Who it genuinely suits

- A barber or stylist in a city with a lot of searching, who needs new faces in the chair.
- Someone starting out, or starting over somewhere new, with no following yet.
- A shop whose clients already use the app and expect to book that way.

It suits you less if most of your bookings come from people who already know you. Then the listing is the door your regulars walk through, and you pay for a door you could own. The ten minute way to find out which group you are in is the count in [is Booksy worth it](/blog/is-booksy-worth-it/): sort your last thirty bookings by how each person found you.

## The questions to ask before you download it

:::copy Questions to answer before you sign up for Booksy Biz

1. How many new clients do I actually need each month, and would search inside an app find them?
2. What is the monthly cost for the number of staff I will have in a year, from Booksy's own site today?
3. What is the card processing rate, and when do payouts reach my bank?
4. If I promote my listing, how exactly is a client counted as new, and what is the fee?
5. Can I export my client list whenever I want, and what is in the file?
6. What happens to my profile, reviews and link if I cancel?

:::

Write the answers down. If you cannot get one, that is an answer too.

## The questions to ask before you leave

Leaving is a bigger move than signing up, because by then your clients have Booksy's link saved, not yours.

1. **Where do my bookings come from now?** Run the thirty booking count. If a real share of new clients still come from search, leaving costs you those.
2. **Have I exported my clients?** Do it first, open the file and check the names, phones and emails came through.
3. **Where will they book instead?** A booking page of your own, with your services, prices and policies, and a link you control. That is the gap BKRDY fills, with the honest trade-off that it is not a marketplace and will not send you new clients.
4. **What stays behind?** Your Booksy reviews, your profile, your link and any cards saved through Booksy's processing.
5. **How will I tell people?** Twice, a week apart, with the new link, and change your Instagram bio the same day.

Keep the old account open one more billing cycle so anyone who booked through the old link still has a slot.

## Questions

### What is Booksy?

Booksy is a booking marketplace for beauty and barber services. Clients use the Booksy app to find businesses nearby and book, and pros use Booksy Biz to manage the calendar, clients and payments behind their listing.

### What is the difference between Booksy and Booksy Biz?

Booksy is the client app, free for clients, used to search and book. Booksy Biz is the business app where a pro sets services, prices and hours, runs the calendar and takes payments. The subscription sits on the Biz side.

### Is the Booksy app free?

For clients, yes. For pros, Booksy Biz is a paid subscription, with costs that grow with staff and add-ons, plus card processing. Prices change, so check Booksy's own site for the current price.
