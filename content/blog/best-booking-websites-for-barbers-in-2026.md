If you are a barber researching booking software, you have probably noticed most platforms competing for your business sell themselves as "all-in-one tools" or "scheduling software." That framing misses the point. What you actually need is a website. A real one, with booking built in. Your clients book through your bio link on Instagram, a link you texted them, or a Google search for your shop. The first thing they see is either your brand or a generic booking form. Whichever shows up has a real effect on whether they tap Book.

We compared six platforms with that lens. Some are full booking software (GlossGenius, Booksy). Some are website builders with booking bolted on (Squarespace + Acuity). Some are barber-specific (Squire, TheCut). And one is built specifically as a designed booking website for beauty businesses, including barbershops (BKRDY).

Here is how they actually compare for a barber in 2026. Competitor prices change often, so treat ours as a guide and check each vendor's own page before you decide.

## What makes a great booking website for a barbershop.

Six criteria we used to evaluate each platform. Each matters for a different reason.

## BKRDY. A real designed website, 0% markup.

**Price.** The Free plan is $0, Solo $18/mo and Studio $29/mo billed annually ($21 and $34 monthly), 0% platform markup. The Free plan needs no card. **Best for.** Barbers who want their site to look like a brand and do not want a platform cut on top of card processing.

BKRDY gives you a designed booking website (templates are named, not generic, with The Fade Room as the natural fit for barbershops) and all the booking, deposit, and reminder features built in. You get online booking, deposits and reminders as you would on GlossGenius or Booksy, but the surface your clients touch is a designed website, not a booking widget.

**What stood out.** Per-chair calendars work cleanly, each barber gets their own login and schedule on the Studio plan, and all payments land in the one shop Stripe account. Deposits at booking are Stripe-backed and the cancellation rules are enforced in the booking flow, not on a paper sign. The Fade Room template is genuinely well-designed (dark, neon, after-hours) and looks like a real shop. 0% platform markup means the only card cost is Stripe's own rate, roughly 2.9% plus 30 cents. Free migration from Booksy, Square, or Acuity. Usually same day. On Squire or anything else, ask first what can come over.

**What is missing.** No native mobile app for owners (web-only, though mobile-optimized). No POS hardware or tap to pay (payment links, saved cards, or Mark paid for cash). No multi-location management.

**Verdict.** Strongest pick for the majority of barbershops, especially shops that care about brand. Try the [The Fade Room template demo](https://thefaderoom.bkrdy.me/) for the cleanest sense of what a BKRDY barbershop site feels like. Or read the dedicated [For barbers](/for-barbers/) page.

## Squire. Barber-only, from $30 a month.

**Price.** $30/mo for an individual barber, $50/mo for a single-location shop, then $150/mo and $250/mo per shop. **Best for.** Barbershops that want booking, POS, and shop management in one platform.

Built specifically for barbershops (no spa, no salon, just barber), Squire puts booking, POS, and shop management into one platform, and the higher plans add commission and rent collection, inventory, and a branded app.

**What stood out.** The shop plans are a flat fee per shop, however many barbers you add. Tap to Pay on iPhone and Android with no extra hardware. Genuinely deep on the operations side.

**What is missing.** The tools that set it apart sit on the higher plans: commission and rent collection at $150/mo, then loyalty, gift cards, and inventory at $250/mo. Branded landing pages are a $25/mo add-on. It sells booking and shop tools, not a designed website of your own.

**Verdict.** Worth a look if you run a full shop and want one platform for everything. The $30 plan suits a solo barber too, without the shop tools.

## TheCut. Marketplace traffic, brand trade-off.

**Price.** Free, or $25/mo for Pro, with 2.75% card processing on both. New clients from its discovery feature cost $10 each on Free and are included on Pro. **Best for.** Barbers who want marketplace-driven client acquisition.

TheCut is a barber app with client discovery, similar to Booksy. The trade-off is the same as any marketplace: you get traffic, but you give up brand control. Your profile is TheCut-branded. Clients book through TheCut, not on your own site.

**What stood out.** Client discovery if you are in a city where TheCut is popular. A free plan with no credit card. Native mobile app for both barbers and clients, with in-app tipping.

**What is missing.** Your profile is TheCut-branded, not yours, and there is no website of your own.

**Verdict.** Solid if you are in a TheCut-heavy market and want the marketplace as your primary client channel. Skip if you have a strong brand and want a real website.

## Booksy. A marketplace listing, with paid promotion on top.

**Price.** $29.99/mo, plus $20/mo per extra team member, plus card processing from 2.49% + 10¢. Optional Booksy Boost takes 30% of a new client's first visit, up to $100. **Best for.** Barbers who use marketplace exposure heavily and benefit from in-app loyalty programs.

Booksy pairs a booking app with a marketplace listing, and every feature is in the one plan: loyalty cards, marketing tools, and native mobile apps for both barbers and clients.

**What stood out.** Marketplace exposure in dense barber communities, especially for newer shops building a client base. Loyalty programs work natively in-app. iOS + Android apps for both sides.

**What is missing.** The bill grows with every barber you add. Better visibility in marketplace search means turning on Boost, and then each new client it brings costs you 30% of their first visit. Your profile is Booksy-branded. No website-builder side.

**Verdict.** Good if marketplace is genuinely driving your bookings. If you get clients from Instagram and word-of-mouth, you are paying for a marketplace you do not need. See our [Booksy alternative comparison](/compare/booksy-alternative/) for the math.

## GlossGenius. Strong POS, its own card rates.

**Price.** $28/mo (Standard), or $24/mo billed annually, with card processing at 2.4% + 15¢ in person and 3.4% + 15¢ online. **Best for.** Barbershops that sell meaningful retail (pomade, beard oil, t-shirts) and need integrated POS.

GlossGenius is a strong contender from the salon and beauty SaaS world. Their booking, payments, and CRM are deep, and their retail and inventory features are best-in-class for the price.

**What stood out.** Full POS with card readers (sold separately) and inventory tracking. Gift cards native. Strong mobile app for owners.

**What is missing.** Templates are generic (you pick a color scheme, not a designed look). Not barber-specific (built for the broader beauty market).

**Verdict.** Strong pick if retail is a real part of your revenue, or if most clients pay at the chair. See our [GlossGenius alternative comparison](/compare/glossgenius-alternative/) for the full picture.

## Setmore. Free, basic, outgrown fast.

**Price.** Free for up to 4 users. Pro is $5 per user a month billed annually, or $12 monthly. **Best for.** Brand-new barbers who need to start booking with $0 upfront.

Setmore is the budget pick. Their free tier handles basic online booking for up to four users. It is not pretty, but it works.

**What stood out.** Genuinely free for a small shop. Integrates with Stripe, Square, and PayPal.

**What is missing.** No designed templates (booking page is functional but bare). No marketplace, no loyalty. Deposits, text reminders, and two-way calendar sync are on Pro, so you will likely outgrow the free plan once no-shows start to cost you.

**Verdict.** Use it to start. Move up once you are booking real volume.

## How to pick the right one for your shop.

If you are trying to decide between these six platforms, the answer usually comes down to one question: **where are your clients coming from?**

**Mostly Instagram, word of mouth, or your existing client list?** BKRDY. Your bio link goes to a real designed site, you pay 0% markup, you own your brand. The right pick for the majority of barbers.

**Mostly marketplace search ("barbers near me")?** Booksy or TheCut, depending on your city. You will pay the fee but you will get the traffic.

**High volume, multi-location, retail-heavy?** Squire (if budget allows) or GlossGenius (if retail is a meaningful revenue line).

**Brand new, no clients yet, need to start at $0?** Setmore or the BKRDY Free plan for the first 90 days. Move to a paid plan as soon as you start booking real volume.

## For most barbers, the answer is BKRDY.

The 0% markup means BKRDY adds nothing on top of Stripe's rate, and the designed template gives you a website instead of a generic booking form.

The rest have a specific need (marketplace exposure, full POS, multi-location) that one of the other platforms handles better. For those shops, pick the platform that matches the specific need.

## Questions

### Do I have to leave my current platform to test BKRDY?

No. BKRDY is free to start, so you can run both while you switch. You can build your BKRDY site, get it ready, then switch your Instagram bio link when you are ready. Your current platform stays running until you cancel.

### Can I run BKRDY and Booksy at the same time?

Yes. Some shops do this to keep marketplace exposure on Booksy while running their own designed website on BKRDY. Two subscriptions, but you keep the marketplace traffic during the transition.

### What does 0% actually save?

The 0% is BKRDY's own markup; Stripe's processing fee, roughly 2.9% plus 30 cents, still applies. So there is only a saving where a platform takes a percentage on top of card processing, like a commission on new clients from a marketplace. Where the percentage is the card processing itself, card costs come out about the same, and the real comparison is the monthly fee and the website.

### I have booth renters. Does BKRDY support that?

Partly, on the Studio plan. Each booth renter gets their own login, hours and services inside the shop site, and sees their own schedule rather than the whole shop's. All payments land in the one shop Stripe account, though, so BKRDY does not split payouts or collect booth rent.

### How long does migration actually take?

From Booksy, GlossGenius, Acuity and the other common platforms, BKRDY handles the migration for you, free, usually the same day. Services, clients and upcoming appointments all come over. On anything else, like Squire, ask first what can come across.

### Can clients still book by phone if they do not use the website?

Yes. You can manually add bookings from your calendar, so the phone option stays there for clients who prefer it.

### What if I want a fully custom website (not just a template)?

BKRDY templates are customizable (your photos, your services, your colors), but they do not go fully custom (custom code, custom layouts). For that you would need a designer plus a booking integration like Acuity. That setup costs more to build and keep running than a flat plan at $18 to $47 a month billed annually for BKRDY.

### Which BKRDY template should a barbershop pick?

The Fade Room is the natural fit for barbershops (dark, neon, late-hours). Blackline is the architectural option (brushed brass, clean lines). Bottega is the warm, handcrafted option. A good start is trying The Fade Room demo at thefaderoom.bkrdy.me.
