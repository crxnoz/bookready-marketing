Decide this now, while nobody is asking. The squeeze-in is the one appointment you always answer under pressure, with a brush in your other hand, and the answer usually turns on how nicely the person asked rather than what it costs you.

Which is why two clients can send the same request in the same week and get different answers, and neither will ever know why.

:::answer
A last minute squeeze-in is safe when three things are true at once: the service is short, the gap is real rather than one you made by shortening someone else, and the client is one you want more of. If any one is missing, the honest answer is a waitlist place, not a yes. Charge a fee on the ones you take and state it before you confirm, because the appointment costs you more to deliver and the fee keeps the request for when it genuinely matters.
:::

## Saying no feels like saying no to them

The request almost never comes from someone unreasonable. It comes from a good client who shows up, tips and sends their friends. They have a real reason: a funeral on Saturday, a flight Friday, a job interview. And they ask the way people ask when they know they are pushing it. Any chance. I know you are probably full.

That phrasing is the whole problem, because no stops sounding like "that time is not available" and starts sounding like "you are not worth it to me". So you say yes, and that is not weakness. What is missing is a rule, not backbone, and without one the slot goes to whoever words the ask best.

## The appointment is 30 minutes. The cost is not.

In the moment you price the service: half an hour, they are quick, fine. That is not what you are spending.

- **The buffer.** The 15 minutes between your 2pm and your 3pm is how you clean down, write the notes and start the next client calm.
- **The lunch.** The squeeze-in lands in the only gap in the day. That is the gap you eat in.
- **Everyone after them.** You start eight minutes late and it compounds. The 6pm client, who did nothing wrong, waits.
- **The precedent.** The expensive one, and it shows up later.

> The client you squeeze in once has not learned that you did them a favour. They have learned that the calendar is negotiable.

Six weeks on they ask again, with less preamble, behaving exactly as you taught them.

## Three conditions, and all three have to be true

![The three conditions that make a last minute squeeze-in safe: a short service, a real gap, and a client you want more of.](/images/blog/the-squeeze-in-three.webp)

Yes only when all three are true. Two out of three is a no.

1. **It is a short service.** A polish change, a brow wax, a fringe trim. Squeezing a full set into a gap is not a squeeze-in, it is a second job that starts at the end of your first one.
2. **There is a real gap.** A cancellation, an unbooked hour, a late start tomorrow. A gap you make by trimming someone else or eating your break is a loan against your own day.
3. **It is a client you want more of.** Someone who shows up, pays and rebooks. This is a judgement and you are allowed to make it. The regular who has never cancelled on you has earned something the serial rescheduler has not.

Three conditions beats a feeling, because you can apply them in nine seconds.

## Charging for it is not greedy

An appointment outside your normal pattern costs more to deliver than one inside it. Pricing it differently is honest, not opportunistic. Anyone with a same-day rate does the same.

The fee does two things, and the second matters more. It pays for the disruption, which is fair. And it means the request only arrives when the client genuinely needs it: a free squeeze-in gets asked for whenever it would be mildly convenient, a $20 one when there is a funeral on Saturday.

Say the fee before you confirm, never after, because a charge explained at the chair reads as a surprise and costs more goodwill than it collects. Take it when you approve rather than on the day, same as any deposit: a short notice no-show costs you the break you gave up for it. The usual [no-show protections](/guides/reducing-no-shows/) apply more here, not less.

## The better answer is usually neither yes nor no

Listen to what is actually being asked. Almost nobody wants 7pm Thursday specifically. They want to come sooner than the appointment they have, and only one of those requests costs you your day.

So answer the real one. Put them on the cancellation list, and when somebody drops out of Wednesday at 11, the slot is theirs. Same client, same week, and now they are delighted rather than squeezed, because a freed slot is a lucky break instead of a favour you quietly pay for. ## Say the no in one sentence

The no is a sentence, not a conversation. Short, no apology tour, no account of how busy you have been, always with a specific alternative attached. "I am fully booked" is a dead end.

:::copy Two replies, ready to send

**The no, with the waitlist**
I am full Friday, so I cannot fit that in. You are top of my cancellation list this week, so if anything frees up you get first offer. Otherwise my next opening is Tuesday at 10, want me to hold it?

**The yes, with the fee**
I can fit you in Thursday at 1pm. It is outside my normal pattern, so there is a $20 short notice fee added to your total. Happy to book it if that works.
:::

Neither one says sorry, and neither explains. You are not confessing to anything.

## Zero is the wrong target

A shop that never bends reads as rigid, and rigid loses the odd client to the person down the road who said yes. That flexibility is part of why people pick a small business over a chain.

So the goal is not zero. It is that the ones you do are chosen rather than extracted, a difference your clients will never notice and your Thursdays will.

## Questions

### What counts as a squeeze-in?

Any appointment you take outside your normal pattern because someone asked at short notice. It is defined by the asking rather than the clock, so an evening slot that sits in your calendar at a set price is just an appointment.

### Should I charge extra for fitting a client in last minute?

Yes, if you want to keep offering it. It costs you more to deliver than a normal appointment, so a fee is fair, and it has a second effect: the request stops arriving for minor convenience. State the fee before you confirm, never afterwards.

### How do I turn down a last minute request without losing the client?

Give the no and an alternative in the same message. Say you are full that day, say they are top of your cancellation list, and name a time you can do. It lands softly because it arrives with something in its hand.

### Is a waitlist better than squeezing people in?

For most of these requests, yes, because the client wants to come sooner, not that exact slot. A waitlist uses a gap that already exists, so your break and the clients after them stay untouched.
