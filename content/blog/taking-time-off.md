You can take a week off without losing clients. The clients who drift during a vacation are almost never lost to the vacation itself. They are lost to the surprise: the regular who tried to book, found nothing, assumed you had gone, and booked somebody else.

So the work is not in the week away. It is in the weeks before it, and most of it takes ten minutes.

:::answer
Pick the week early, close those dates on your calendar before anyone books them, and tell your regulars with a specific date they can book for when you are back. Move anyone already booked yourself, set an out-of-office on your messages, and fill your return week before you leave, so it is booked the day you get back.
:::

## Pick the week early, and pick it on purpose

Six to eight weeks ahead is a comfortable lead for most books. It is far enough out that few people are booked in yet, and close enough that you will actually go.

Pick a week that is quiet for you, not quiet for everyone. For many stylists and nail techs that is the stretch after a holiday rush. For a massage therapist with a lot of office workers it might be the opposite. Look at last year's calendar if you have it and find the week with the most gaps.

Avoid the week right before your busy season. Clients who cannot get in before an event they care about are the ones who try someone new.

## Close the dates before anyone books them

This is the step that does the most. The moment you decide, mark the days off on your booking calendar. Every day the dates stay open is a day someone might book into your vacation, and then you have a conversation instead of a holiday.

Include the edges. If you fly out Friday evening, close Friday afternoon. If you land late on Sunday, think hard about Monday morning. The first day back is not the day for your longest service.

In BKRDY, days you mark as time off close booking for those dates, and the calendar shows which existing bookings fall inside them so you can move each one. Whatever you use, check that it lets you close dates in advance before you plan around it.

## Move anyone already booked, personally

If someone is already booked in that week, contact them yourself, by name, with two specific alternatives. Do not cancel the appointment and let a system email do the explaining. A cancellation email from a business you like reads as something going wrong.

Offer a time before you go or a time after you get back, whichever they prefer. Most people are fine with a week's move when they hear it from you first.

## Tell your regulars, with a date they can book

The message to your regulars has one job: give them somewhere to land. "I am away the week of the 12th" leaves them to work out what to do. "I am away the week of the 12th, and I have opened the 20th and 21st for anyone who usually comes around then" tells them.

Send it about a month ahead, by direct message to the people you see most often, and put the same dates on your booking page and your bio. A story disappears in a day.

:::copy Time off message to clients
Hi Jordan, a heads-up so you can plan: I am taking a week off, Monday the 12th to Sunday the 18th.

If you are usually due around then, I can see you before I go (I have the 8th and 9th) or as soon as I am back (the 20th and 21st are open now).

Reply with what suits you and I will book it in. Thank you for being so easy to work with.
:::

## Set the out-of-office

![A checklist for taking a week off without losing clients: pick the week early, close the dates before anyone books them, move anyone already booked, tell your regulars with a date they can book, set the out-of-office, and book your return week before you go.](/images/blog/taking-time-off-week-off-night.webp)

People will still message while you are away. Set an automatic reply on your email and, if your phone allows it, on your texts and messages. Keep it short: when you are back, and how to book in the meantime.

Clients can still book from your booking page while you are away, for dates after you return, as long as those dates are open. That is the point of the out-of-office: it sends them there instead of leaving them waiting for a reply.

Then leave your phone alone. A week off where you answer booking messages every evening is a week of working from a different chair.

## Book your return week before you go

The first week back is where a vacation quietly costs money. Clients who meant to book did not, the gaps are bigger than usual, and you spend the week rebuilding.

Fill it before you leave. Every client who sits in your chair in the two weeks before your break gets asked whether they want their next appointment booked now, for after you return. Rebooking in the chair is the habit that makes this easy, and [how to rebook clients](/guides/how-to-rebook-clients/) covers the words.

A waitlist helps with the rest. If someone cancels in your first week back, a client who wanted sooner gets the time instead of it sitting empty.

## The trade-off is real, and worth taking

A week off costs you a week of income and possibly a client or two who would not wait. That cost is real, and it is the reason so many independent pros never take one.

The other side is the body. A barber standing all day, a nail tech bent over a desk, a massage therapist using their whole frame as the tool: none of that repairs itself on Sundays alone. A week away now costs less than the month you lose to an injury later. And a client who comes every few weeks can almost always move by one week, which is all you are asking of them.

If your book is still thin, a full week may cost more than you can carry right now. Take two long weekends instead, and use the same steps for each.

## What to do this week

1. Pick a week six to eight weeks out.
2. Close those dates on your calendar today.
3. Write the message to your regulars and save it to send a month ahead.
4. Ask every client this week whether they want to book for after you are back.

## Questions

### How far in advance should I tell clients I am taking time off?

Close the dates on your calendar as soon as you decide, and send the message about a month ahead. The calendar matters more, because it stops new bookings landing in the week you are away.

### Will I lose clients if I take a vacation?

Rarely, if they hear it from you with a date they can book. The clients who go elsewhere are usually the ones who tried to book, found nothing and heard nothing.

### Should I cancel appointments that fall in my time off?

Move them, personally, rather than cancelling. Contact each client by name with two alternatives, one before you go and one after you are back.
