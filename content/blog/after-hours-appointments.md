You already take appointments outside your hours. Almost everyone in this trade does. The question is whether you ever actually decided to, or whether it happened to you one yes at a time.

That is the difference between a late night that pays well and a Tuesday that finishes at nine.

:::answer
Taking appointments outside your opening hours is fine when it is a decision and a problem when it is a favour. There are three good versions: don't do it at all, open one fixed late night a week that anyone can book in the normal calendar, or make outside-hours times bookable at a premium stated on your booking page. Whichever you pick, put it in the calendar once so the slot is either bookable or it is not. What burns owners out is not the evening work, it is being asked every week and deciding every week.
:::

## It starts as an exception and it never stays one

The first one is always reasonable. A good client got a new job and cannot do daytime. A regular asks nicely for next Thursday at seven, and saying no feels mean when your evening is empty anyway.

So you say yes. It goes fine. Then they book the same thing six weeks later, because why would they not. A friend of theirs hears about it. Nobody is doing anything wrong. They are asking a reasonable question of someone who already answered it once.

## The appointment is not the cost

The appointment is the easy part. You like the work, the client is often your nicest one, and the money is real.

What it costs is this. Your day stops having an end, so you never fully put it down. Nobody else is in the building. And the evening you were going to use for the orders and the messages is now a service, so that work moves to Sunday.

> Every informal evening is really two appointments: the one in the chair, and the one they book next time because you said yes to this one.

Be fair about the other side. For plenty of owners the evening is the best hour of the week: quiet shop, no walk-ins, no phone, a client who is not squeezing you in. If that is you, run it properly instead of accidentally.

## Three ways to run it on purpose

### Not at all

Hours are hours. The booking page closes when the shop closes, and there is nothing to ask for.

The cleanest option, and the right one for anyone whose evenings are already spoken for: kids to collect, a second job, a class, or a limit you found the hard way. The first few times someone asks is a script problem, not a scheduling one.

### A fixed late night

One evening a week, open to everyone, sitting in the normal calendar like any other block. Thursdays until nine. Done.

Easiest to explain and the fairest, because nobody has to ask for a favour and nobody gets something another client could not have. It keeps the other four evenings yours. Pick the night your clients actually want, usually Thursday rather than Monday, and fill it with your longer services. A night that fills with three 20 minute appointments is not worth being there for.

### Priced after hours slots

Outside-hours times stay bookable and carry a premium. An hour of your Thursday evening is worth more than an hour of your Tuesday afternoon, because you are giving up something that is not for sale during the day.

This only works if the price is on the booking page before anyone picks a time. Stated up front it is just a price and nobody blinks. Explained afterwards, at the chair or in a message, it reads as a surprise charge, so never retro-fit it onto a client who has already booked. Charging more for an evening is normal, not cheeky: every business with a weekend rate does the same.

## Not everyone should be allowed to book 9pm

The honest answer to who can take these slots is usually not everyone.

- **Regulars only.** Clients who have been in before, whose name you know, who show up for what they book.
- **Longer services only.** If you are staying late, stay late for a full set or a colour, not a polish change.
- **Nobody, without asking.** Keep the slot closed and open it by hand when someone you trust asks.

A 9pm Sunday slot a brand new client can book from Instagram at eleven in the morning will eventually be a no-show at 9pm on a Sunday, and that hurts in a way a Tuesday no-show does not. If you open evenings widely, take a deposit. The usual [no-show protections](/guides/reducing-no-shows/) matter more here, not less.

## Working alone at night is not the same as working alone at noon

Working alone outside your hours is different from working alone in the day: the street is quieter, the other units are shut, and nobody is expecting you to be there. Tell someone where you are and when you expect to finish, and keep the door locked between clients. If an evening booking from a name you do not know feels off, move it to a daytime slot without giving a reason.

## Put the decision in the calendar, not in the conversation

The rule that makes any of this survivable: the after hours slot is bookable, or it is not.

If it is bookable, it is in the calendar with a price and a rule about who can take it. If it is not, it is closed, and the answer is the same every time, so the question stops coming.

What wears through is the middle: weighing up whether this person, this week, is worth it, and feeling slightly bad either way. A dozen small decisions a month about the same thing is what makes owners quietly resent clients they like. It shows up in every [scheduling](/blog/lash-artist-scheduling-best-practices/) problem that is really a rules problem.

Open the calendar this week and do one of two things: add a fixed evening block with a price and a note on who can book it, or close the hours and write your two-sentence no somewhere you can copy it from.

## Questions

### Should I charge more for appointments outside my opening hours?

Yes, if you want to keep offering them. An hour of your evening costs you more than an hour of your afternoon. The premium just has to be visible on your booking page before the client picks a time. Charging it afterwards costs more goodwill than the money is worth.

### How do I say no to an evening appointment without upsetting a good client?

Say no and offer something in the same breath. "I don't do evenings, but I hold my first morning slot for people who work, so I can give you Tuesday at eight." Say it the same way every time, so it reads as how you work rather than a judgement on who is asking.

### Is one fixed late night better than taking evenings as they come?

For most owners, yes. One evening a week in the normal calendar is easier to explain and fairer to clients who would never think to ask for a favour, and it keeps the other four evenings yours. It works because you decide once instead of every week.
