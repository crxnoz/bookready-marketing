Vagaro Pro is the professional side of Vagaro: the part owners and staff work in to run the calendar, take payments and keep client records. The other side is the consumer one, where clients browse businesses and book a time. If you came here looking for the business half of Vagaro, that is it.

:::answer
Vagaro Pro is the business-facing side of Vagaro, the side owners and staff use to manage appointments, take payments, and handle clients, marketing, retail, classes and payroll. The consumer side is where clients discover businesses and book. Pro earns its complexity in a multi-chair salon or spa with a front desk and retail or memberships. For one person who wanted a booking page and a deposit, it is more product than the job needs.
:::

## Vagaro has two sides, and Pro is the one you work in

Clients see one Vagaro: somewhere to search for a business near them and book. Owners and staff see another: schedules, checkout, client history, reports, settings. Vagaro Pro is that second side, and it works on a computer as well as on phones and tablets, so a stylist can check her own column between clients. There is no separate company and no hidden tier behind the word Pro. It is the staff-facing half of the same platform.

## The breadth is the point, and the breadth is the learning curve

Vagaro aims at the whole operation rather than just bookings: calendars for a team, card payments and checkout, client records and notes, email and text marketing, inventory and retail for what you sell at the desk, classes and memberships, and payroll.

A product that covers checkout, stock and payroll has to ask you about checkout, stock and payroll. Setup takes longer, and more things end up configured slightly wrong in a way nobody notices for a month.

> The same product that can run a twelve-person salon still asks twelve-person salon questions of one person with one chair.

## Who gets the most out of it

Vagaro is at its best in a business like this:

- Four or more people on the calendar, with different services, different hours and different pay.
- Retail at the front desk, with stock you count and reorder.
- Classes, courses or memberships, which bill on a schedule instead of per visit.
- A front desk, or at least one person whose job includes keeping the system tidy.

That last one matters more than owners expect: somebody has to merge duplicate client records, fix the service still set to 45 minutes when you now take an hour, and notice the add-on nobody opened since spring. With a receptionist the depth pays off, and one login holds the schedule, the till, the stock and the payroll.

## Who finds it heavy, said fairly

A solo lash artist who wanted online booking and a 50% deposit ends up with more settings than she will ever open. Same for a barber with one chair, or a nail tech working out of a spare room. Nothing is broken. The depth that serves a twelve-person salon is overhead for one person, and overhead gets paid in evenings.

The tell is what you do in a normal week. If it is "look at tomorrow, take a payment, text somebody back", you are using a small corner of something large and paying for the rest. Something lighter fits better. BKRDY is one, and so are others in the [honest comparison of salon booking software](/blog/salon-booking-software-comparison/).

## The bill grows with your team and with what you switch on

The shape of the cost, without pretending to know today's figures: a base subscription that grows with the number of staff on the calendar, plus add-ons you turn on, plus card processing on what you take. Prices change and get rearranged, so check Vagaro's own pricing page rather than trusting a number in any article, including this one.

The structure matters more than the figure. Two things push the bill up: hiring, and saying yes to a module. Switching on an add-on in March is a decision you have forgotten by June, and it keeps billing. Staff leave too, and the seat does not always leave with them. So read your own invoice line by line once a year.

:::copy Once a year, read your own invoice
Platform review

1. Staff seats. List every seat the subscription pays for, by name. Cross off anyone who left or whose chair has been empty for a month.
2. Add-ons. List every add-on that is switched on, with its monthly cost beside it.
3. Usage. For each add-on, name one thing it did for you this quarter. If you cannot name one, mark it cut.
4. Processing. What you paid in card fees last month against what you took in.
5. Total. Base plus seats plus add-ons plus processing, next to the same number from last year.
6. Act. Turn off one thing today, before the next bill runs.
:::

## The marketplace is worth real money right up until it is not

Vagaro has a consumer-facing side where clients discover businesses they have never used. When your Thursday afternoons are empty that is genuinely valuable: somebody searching for a brow wax near them finds you with no effort on your part.

Once your book is full, the same thing is a cost you keep paying. Regulars start arriving through it, so you are paying for an introduction to people who already have your number. You stopped needing the part that was doing the work.

## What to check before you commit

Five things, all easier to ask before you sign than after:

1. Whether you can export your client list yourself, today, from inside the product: names, emails, phone numbers. Not "support will send it over".
2. What happens to your booking link if you leave. Clients have it saved, and it is in your Instagram bio.
3. Whether you can use your own domain, so the address clients type belongs to you.
4. Which add-ons are on by default or during a trial, and which you will still use in month four.
5. How many seats you are agreeing to, and what the bill does when somebody leaves mid-month.

If you do move later, the [step by step for leaving Vagaro](/guides/migrate-from-vagaro/) covers the order.

If you run a team with retail, classes or memberships and somebody keeps the system tidy, Vagaro earns its complexity. If you are one person who wanted a booking page that looks like your business and takes a deposit, a lighter product fits better, and the [roundup of Vagaro alternatives](/blog/vagarocom-alternatives/) names the usual options. That is not a mark against Vagaro. It is built for a bigger business than yours.

## Questions

### What is Vagaro Pro used for?

Running a beauty business: the appointment calendar, card payments, client records and notes, marketing, and retail, classes, memberships and payroll. Clients never see it, they use the consumer side to book.

### Is Vagaro Pro a separate product from Vagaro?

No. It is the professional half of the same platform, used by owners and staff on a computer or a phone. The consumer half is where clients browse and book.

### Is Vagaro worth it for a solo beauty professional?

Often not, for a structural reason rather than a quality one. It is built to run a whole operation, and one person who needs booking, reminders and a deposit uses a small corner and pays for the rest.

### How does Vagaro's pricing work?

A base subscription that grows with the number of staff on the calendar, add-ons you switch on, and card processing. Prices change, so check Vagaro's own page. The bill rises when you hire and when you enable a module.
