A nail tech portfolio does one job: it lets a stranger decide you can do the nails they want, before they have met you. Everything in it should help them make that decision quickly.

That means less than most new techs think. A dozen strong, clearly labelled sets beat a hundred photos nobody can sort through.

:::answer
Show the range of services you want to be booked for, with each set labelled by its service name, in the same light and background. Keep it on your booking site, in Instagram highlights sorted by service, and on your phone for in-person questions. Before you have clients, build it from friends, nail models and practice, and say so honestly.
:::

## What to include

Think about what a client is trying to find out.

- **Every service you sell.** If you offer gel, builder gel and acrylic, show each. A client who wants acrylic will not book from a page of gel manicures.
- **Short and long.** Many clients want short, wearable nails. Show those as well as the long sets that get the likes.
- **Simple and detailed.** A clean single color proves your prep and shape. Nail art proves your hand. Show both.
- **Different hands.** Different skin tones and nail shapes show you can do the set on them, not just on one model.
- **How it holds up.** A photo of a set at two or three weeks, still neat at the fill, says more about your work than another fresh one.

Leave out sets you are not proud of, and the styles you do not want to do again.

## How many

Enough to show the range, then stop. Around two to four sets per service is plenty to start. Replace weaker photos as better ones come in, so the portfolio gets stronger rather than longer.

## Label every set

A photo with no label makes people message you to ask what it is. Under each set, use the exact name from your price list: "Builder gel overlay, short" or "Acrylic full set with French". Now the photo works as a menu. Someone sees it and knows what to book.

Add the time and price when they help. A client who sees that the set they love takes two hours and costs what they expected is ready to book, not just to save the post.

:::copy Label for each set in your portfolio
[Service name exactly as on your price list], [length]
About [time] · from $[price]
Book it: [your booking link]
:::

## Where to keep it

**Your booking site.** The gallery on your booking page is where people go straight from your link to booking. On BKRDY, the gallery holds six photos on the Free plan and more on paid plans, so pick your strongest six and keep them current.

**Instagram highlights, by service.** One highlight for gel, one for acrylic, one for art. A visitor taps the service they want instead of scrolling your whole feed.

**Your phone.** An album of your best sets for when someone at the counter or at a party asks what you do. It is a portfolio too.

## Building one before you have clients

Everyone starts with an empty portfolio. Fill it honestly.

- **Friends and family.** Do their nails and photograph them properly.
- **Nail models.** People who get a set at a reduced price in return for photos and patience. Agree on the price and the photos before the appointment.
- **Practice on tips.** Fine for showing nail art and color, but label it as tips. A client should never think a press-on display is a set on a real hand.

Take every photo the same way: daylight, a plain background, the same poses. The [guide to taking nail pictures](/blog/how-to-take-pictures-of-nails/) has the setup.

## Nail tech portfolio examples

Three ways to arrange it, depending on what you do.

1. **By service.** Gel, builder gel, acrylic, pedicures. Best when your menu is broad.
2. **By length and style.** Short and natural, medium, long and statement. Best when clients choose by look.
3. **By occasion.** Everyday, events, holidays. Best when your clients book for something specific.

Pick one and keep it. A portfolio sorted three ways at once is harder to use than one sorted simply.

## The trade-off

Showing only your most elaborate sets gets attention, and it can bring you bookings you do not want: long art sets from clients who expect them for the price of a simple manicure. Showing only safe, simple work undersells you.

Show what you want more of, priced where the client can see it, and the portfolio does the filtering for you.

## Do this week

Pick your six best sets. Label each with its service name, put them in your booking site's gallery, and make one Instagram highlight per service you offer.

## Questions

### What should a nail tech portfolio include?

Every service you sell, short and long sets, simple and detailed work, different hands, and a set photographed at the fill. Label each with the service name from your price list.

### How do I build a nail tech portfolio with no clients?

Photograph sets on friends, family and nail models, and practice art on tips labelled as tips. Use the same light and background every time so the portfolio looks consistent.

### Where should I put my nail portfolio?

On your booking site, in Instagram highlights sorted by service, and in an album on your phone for people who ask in person.
