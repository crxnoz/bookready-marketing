Open this week in your calendar. Appointments, other people's names, in other people's chosen hours. Everything else you do to keep the business running is in your head, and your head is not a calendar.

So it happens in the cracks. You order product at 11pm because you noticed you were down to two bottles. You answer booking questions between clients with one glove off. You photograph nothing, because the good light was at 2pm and you were working. An owner who is busy every hour and behind on everything is looking at a calendar that holds only other people.

:::answer
The hours in your week that are not appointments are still working hours, and anything not in the calendar gets sold to someone else. Pick the two or three kinds of non client work that cost you most when they slip, give each a specific hour, and mark that time unbookable so your booking page cannot offer it. A block you will give away the first time someone asks nicely is a preference, not a block.
:::

## The work nobody books and nobody pays you for directly

Name it concretely, because vague work is easy to skip. Non appointment hours go to:

- **Restocking and ordering.** Counting what is low, placing it, unpacking it.
- **Laundry, sanitising and sterilising.** Towels, capes, tools, bed roll.
- **Replying.** Booking requests, questions, the client who wants to move to Friday.
- **Invoicing and chasing.** Balances owed, receipts, the books.
- **Photographing your work and posting it.** Two usable shots, then the caption.
- **Training and practice.** The technique you paid for in March and have used on nobody.
- **Maintenance.** The bulb, the sticky drawer, the deep clean behind the station.
- **Eating.**

All real work. Nobody is billed for any of it.

> Work that nobody pays for directly will always lose an argument with work that does.

## Pick two or three, and give them an hour

Do not block all of it. Nine blocks in a week is a fantasy you abandon by Thursday.

Pick the two or three that cost you most when they slip. Not the most virtuous, the ones with a price: running out of adhesive mid set, a booking request that sat four days and went to the girl down the road, a quarter of receipts to reconstruct. Give each one a specific hour on a specific day, and mark it unbookable rather than hoping nobody asks.

BKRDY lets you mark time unbookable on specific dates and set the hours inside which a day can be booked at all, which is the difference between a block and a note to yourself. Whatever you use, the [setting](/features/) matters less than the hour no longer being offered.

My blocks, week of ________

1. Admin and messages: ________ , 45 minutes, twice a week. A low energy hour, mid afternoon, never my sharpest one.
2. Ordering and restock: ________ , 30 minutes, same day every week, early.

The rule: these hours are bookable to nobody. If I move one, it goes to another hour the same week. I do not delete it.
:::

## Each kind of work has an hour that suits it

Where you put a block decides whether it survives.

- **Admin: a low energy hour.** Replying does not need your best work, and your best hour is worth more with a client in it.
- **Photos: straight after the work that photographs well.** Not at the end of the day, when the set is two services old and you want to go home. Fifteen minutes behind the one appointment a week you would show off, plus [somewhere to use them](/guides/content-ideas-for-beauty-businesses/).
- **Ordering: the same day every week.** The sameness matters more than the day. Running out of product is rarely a money problem, it is a nobody-owned-Tuesday one.
- **Practice: your quietest season, scheduled now.** Not "when things calm down", because they will not. If you are slow in January, that is when you learn the new service, and it goes in the calendar in November.

## The block almost nobody sets is the end of the day

If your last bookable slot ends at the time you want to leave, you will never leave at that time. The day runs over, payment takes a minute, she wants to rebook, the station needs wiping, and it is forty minutes past.

So make the last stretch unbookable. Thirty minutes, forty if your services run long. A late day runs into it instead of into your evening, and an on time day buys you a proper clean or an early exit. People resist this one hardest, because it looks like refusing money. It is the difference between closing at six and closing whenever the day lets you.

## You never have to explain what the block is

Never tell a client that Tuesday at two is your ordering hour. The moment a block has a name, it becomes negotiable.

"I do not have anything that day, but I can do Thursday at two" is a complete sentence. And if your booking page never shows the slot, the conversation does not start.

## Blocks cost you bookable hours, and in a slow month you will want them back

Four hours of blocks a week is up to four appointments you are choosing not to take. In a slow month you will want those hours back, and that instinct is right.

The mistake is how most people act on it. They let the block erode, one "just this once" at a time, until it is gone and they cannot say when it went.

Do it deliberately instead. Release it for a stated stretch: the Tuesday admin hour is open for all of February and comes back in March. Decide where that work moves to before you open the slot. If the answer every month is that you need the hours for clients, that is a capacity problem, not a time blocking one, and the fix is price or [a second pair of hands](/guides/growing-a-salon/).

## Look at the blocks once a season, not every week

A block you have never once used should go. You were wrong about needing it, and an unused block teaches you to ignore the rest.

A block that constantly overruns needs to be longer. If forty five minutes of admin always becomes ninety, it is a ninety minute job.

Check them once a season, while you are already looking at your hours and prices. Agonising every Sunday night is one more unpaid hour.

## Questions

### What should I block first if I only block one thing?

The end of your day. Make the last thirty minutes unbookable and nothing else about your schedule changes, but you stop finishing late every night.

### What do I tell a client who asks for a time I have blocked?

Nothing about the block. "I do not have anything that day, but I can do Thursday at two" is enough, and it is true. Explain that the hour is for ordering and you have invited a negotiation you will lose.

### What if a slow month means I need those hours back?

Release it on purpose and say for how long: open it for February, bring it back in March, and decide where the work goes meanwhile. What you avoid is it disappearing one favour at a time, leaving you neither the hours nor the work.
