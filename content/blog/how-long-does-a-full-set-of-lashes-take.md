How long a full set of lashes takes depends on two things: the style, and the hands doing it. A classic set goes faster than volume, and a lash tech in their first months takes longer than one with a few hundred sets behind them.

Both matter when you book, because a set that runs over pushes every appointment after it.

:::answer
Many lash artists book a classic full set at around two hours, a hybrid set a little longer, and a volume set longer again. A fill is usually around an hour, booked every two to three weeks. As a newer lash tech, time your own sets and book what you actually take, not what experienced artists quote.
:::

## Full set times, by style

These are the ranges lash artists commonly book. Treat them as a starting point and replace them with your own times.

| Service | What it is | Commonly booked |
|---|---|---|
| Classic full set | One extension on each natural lash | Around 2 hours |
| Hybrid full set | A mix of classic and volume fans | Around 2 to 2.5 hours |
| Volume full set | Handmade fans of several fine extensions | Around 2.5 to 3 hours |
| Fill | Replacing extensions that have shed or grown out | Around 1 hour |

A newer lash tech should expect to take longer on every row, sometimes much longer. That is normal, and it is why booking from your own timing matters.

## Time your own sets

For your next ten sets, write down the real time from when the client lies down to when they sit up, and which style it was. Then book each service at your real average plus a little room.

Your times will fall as you get faster. Check them again every month or two and shorten the booking when your average comes down. Booking a set for two hours when you take three makes every client after it wait, and rushing the end of a set shows in the retention.

## Fill vs full set

A fill tops up a set that is still mostly there: you remove the extensions that have grown out or twisted, then add new ones where natural lashes have shed. A full set starts from bare lashes.

Fills are faster and cost less because most of the work is already on the eye. That only holds while enough extensions remain. Many lash artists set a rule that a fill needs a certain share of the set still on, and charge a full set below it. Whatever your rule is, put it in your policies so nobody is surprised at the appointment.

:::copy Fill policy wording
Fills are for sets with at least [amount] of the extensions still on, within [number] weeks of your last appointment. If more has shed, I'll book the time and price of a full set instead. Not sure? Send me a photo before your appointment.
:::

## How often to book fills

Most clients book fills every two to three weeks. Lashes shed naturally, and past about three weeks there is often too little left for a fill. The [article on refill scheduling](/blog/how-refill-scheduling-works-in-a-lash-business/) goes into the cycle in more detail.

The easiest way to keep that rhythm is to book the next fill before the client leaves. "Same time in two and a half weeks?" gets the date in the calendar while they are still looking in the mirror.

## How long a full set lasts

A set does not last a fixed time, because natural lashes shed and grow on their own cycle and the extensions go with them. That is why fills exist. Aftercare matters too: oil-based products near the eyes, rubbing and sleeping face down all shorten how well a set holds.

## Book the time, not just the service

Every service on your booking page should take up its real length in your day. On BKRDY each service has its own length, so a volume full set and a fill block out different amounts of time. Leave a few minutes between appointments to reset the bed and clean up; the [piece on buffer time](/blog/buffer-time-between-appointments/) covers how much.

## The trade-off

Booking long appointments while you learn means fewer clients a day and less money per hour for a while. Booking short to fit more in means running late, rushing, and sets that do not hold, which costs you the clients who would have come back.

Book the time you take. Speed comes with practice, and your booking times can follow it.

## Questions

### How long does a full set of lashes take?

Many lash artists book a classic full set at around two hours, hybrid a little longer and volume longer again. A newer lash tech will usually take longer, so time your own sets.

### What is the difference between a lash fill and a full set?

A full set starts from bare lashes. A fill tops up a set that is still mostly there, so it takes less time and costs less. When too much of the set has shed, a fill becomes a full set.

### How often should you get lash fills?

Most clients book fills every two to three weeks, because natural shedding leaves too little for a fill after that.
