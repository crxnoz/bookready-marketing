Every new lash tech needs eyes to practice on, and the people who lend them are lash models. A model gets a set for less than the full price. You get practice, photos for your portfolio, and timing you can trust before you charge full price.

The arrangement works when both sides know the deal before the first lash goes on.

:::answer
Find lash models among friends of friends, local community groups and your own Instagram stories, and say plainly that you are new and the set will take longer. Charge a small model price that covers your supplies, take a deposit so models show up, and agree on photos in writing before the appointment. Move a model to your real price once your timing and results are steady.
:::

## Why models matter even after training

Training gives you the method. Models give you the hours. The difference between your first ten sets and your fiftieth is speed, consistency and how the lashes hold up over the next few weeks, and only practice on real people gets you there.

Models also give you the first photos for your page, before anyone pays full price to trust you. And when a model comes back for a fill, you learn how your sets hold up over the weeks in between, which no training day can show you.

## Where to find lash models

Start close and move outward.

- **Friends of friends.** Your own friends will say yes, but they are often too polite to tell you what went wrong. Their friends are more honest.
- **Your Instagram stories.** A story saying you are taking a few lash models this month, with what it costs and what it involves, reaches people who already follow you.
- **Local community groups.** Neighborhood and town groups often allow a post like this. Read the group's rules first.
- **People who already wear extensions.** They know what a good set feels like and will give useful feedback.

Say "lash models needed" in plain words, and say you are new. People who answer knowing that are the right models.

:::copy Post asking for lash models
I'm looking for 5 lash models this month while I build my portfolio. Classic full set for $[supply price] instead of $[full price]. Expect around three hours, and I'll take photos at the end. Message me or book here: [your booking link].
:::

## What to charge

Free sounds generous, but free models cancel more and value the set less. A small price that covers your supplies sets a better tone.

Some techs charge a model price for the first set and a model price for one fill, then full price after. Others offer a lower price for a fixed number of visits. Pick one rule, write it down, and apply it to every model.

Take a deposit when they book. A model who has paid something shows up. If you are pricing for the first time, work from your real costs; the same sum in [how to price nails as a beginner](/blog/how-to-price-nails-as-a-beginner/) works for lashes too.

## What models agree to before the appointment

Agree on these before the day, in writing, so nobody is surprised.

:::copy Lash model agreement
Thank you for being a lash model. Here's what to expect:
- The appointment takes longer than usual while I'm learning, so please plan for around three hours.
- The price is $[model price], with a $[deposit] deposit to book that comes off the total.
- I'll photograph your lashes at the end and may post the photos. Tell me now if you'd rather I didn't show your face.
- Tell me before we start about any allergies, eye conditions or past reactions.
- If you need to cancel, please give me [number] hours' notice so I can offer the time to someone else.
:::

Follow your training's guidance on consultations and patch testing for every model, the same as for a paying client.

## When to move a model to full price

A model arrangement has an end. Move a model to your real price when your timing for that service is close to what you will book clients for, and your sets are holding well until the fill.

Tell them ahead of time. "Your next visit will be at my regular price, and I'd love to keep you as a client" turns a model into a regular more often than you would expect.

## The trade-off

Models are not free practice. They cost supplies, time and photos you would have taken anyway, and a model price can stick in people's minds as your price.

Keep the model offer small and time-limited, and keep it off your main price list. Your price list should show what you charge.

## Do this week

Write your model rule: the price, how many visits, the deposit and the photo terms. Post one story asking for three models, and book them at times you can give a long appointment without rushing.

## Questions

### How much should I charge lash models?

Enough to cover your supplies, with a deposit to book. Free models tend to cancel more. Pick one rule and apply it to every model.

### Where can I find lash models?

Friends of friends, your Instagram stories, local community groups, and people who already wear lash extensions. Say plainly that you are new and the appointment will take longer.

### How long should a lash model appointment take?

Longer than a paid appointment while you are learning. Tell models the time to expect before they book, so they can plan around it.
