Releasing your calendar in drops means opening a batch of dates on a set day and time, instead of keeping the whole calendar open all the time. It suits artists whose demand is bigger than their supply, and it changes who gets in: whoever is online when the drop opens, unless you deliberately give your regulars first pick.

For a fully booked nail artist, lash artist or barber, an always-open calendar fails in a particular way. Every slot that frees up goes within minutes to whoever happens to be refreshing, and the calendar stays full months out. Drops replace that with one predictable moment.

:::answer
Appointment drops open a set batch of dates at an announced time, for example the next four weeks every first Monday at 9am. They suit booked-out artists because they turn constant refreshing into one event, but they reward whoever is online at that moment rather than your most loyal clients. Book your regulars before each drop, and announce it the same way every time.
:::

## What a drop is, and what it is not

With an always-open calendar, dates roll into view one day at a time. Every morning a new day appears at the far end of your booking window, and anyone checking at the right moment gets it.

With drops, nothing beyond the current batch is bookable until you open the next one. Then, at a time you announced, a whole block appears at once: two weeks, a month, sometimes more.

That is different from simply shortening how far ahead clients can book, which [how far ahead to open your books](/blog/how-far-ahead-to-open-your-books/) covers. A short window keeps rolling. A drop opens in steps, on a day you choose.

## Who drops suit, and who they do not

Drops make sense when demand is genuinely bigger than supply:

- Your calendar is full for weeks, and every cancellation is claimed almost at once.
- You get regular messages asking when you have openings.
- You want one fixed day to look at your month before it fills, rather than finding out it already has.

They make little sense if you have open days this week. A drop on a thin book is a barrier, not a system. A client who cannot see an opening will book somebody who shows one. If you take a handful of bookings a week, keep the calendar open and put the effort into filling it.

## What drops do to your regulars

This is the trade-off to take seriously. A drop is first come, first served. It rewards the client who is online at 9am on a Monday, not the client who has come every three weeks for two years. A regular who works a shift, has a newborn or simply forgets can be shut out of their usual slot by someone they have never met.

Left alone, that is how drops lose good clients. The fix is to make sure regulars never need the drop at all:

- **Book them at the chair.** Before a regular leaves, book their next appointment by hand, into dates the public drop has not opened yet. They get first pick without competing.
- **Give them a head start** if you prefer: message your regulars a day before the drop opens publicly.
- **Keep a waitlist for the drop that filled**, so a cancellation goes to someone who wanted in rather than to whoever checks next.

Done this way, the drop is how new and occasional clients get in, and your regulars barely notice it exists.

## How to announce a drop

The whole value of a drop is that clients can predict it. So announce it the same way, on the same day, at the same time, every time:

- On your booking page, in plain words, so anyone who finds no dates knows why.
- In your bio, with the date of the next drop.
- In a post or story a day or two before, and again when it opens.
- In what you send new clients after they book, so they learn the rhythm from the start.

Never move the day. A drop that opens on the first Monday one month and a random Thursday the next teaches clients to refresh constantly, which is exactly what you set out to stop.

:::copy Drop announcement for your booking page and bio
New dates open on the first Monday of each month at 9am and cover the next four weeks.

If the date you want is not showing, it is not taken. It just has not opened yet.

Next drop: Monday the 4th at 9am. Cannot make it? Join the waitlist and you will hear if something frees up.

Already a regular? Book your next one before you leave and you will never need the drop.
:::

## The four ways new times can open

Booking systems differ in how many of these they offer. BKRDY has four ways new times open, and they are worth comparing whichever tool you use:

1. **All the time.** The calendar rolls open within your booking window. The simplest, and right for most books.
2. **On a schedule.** A new batch opens at the same time each week or month. The predictable kind of drop.
3. **In drops.** You pick the day a batch of dates opens, so you can time it around holidays, travel or a busy season.
4. **Only dates you open.** Nothing opens unless you open it on the calendar yourself. For guest spots, pop-ups or a book that runs entirely by invitation.

On a schedule and in drops differ mainly in who decides the next date: the calendar, the same way every time, or you, each time. If you want clients to learn one rhythm, a fixed schedule is easier to keep. If your weeks vary a lot, picking each drop gives you control and costs you a little predictability.

## Decide three things before your first drop

- **How many weeks each drop opens.** Enough to carry you to the next drop, with a few days of overlap so nobody hits an empty stretch.
- **What time of day.** A quiet morning when you can answer messages for an hour afterwards.
- **How regulars get in.** Decide before the first drop, not after the first complaint.

Then run three drops before you judge it. The first one is always messy, because nobody knows the rhythm yet, you included.

## Questions

### What are appointment drops?

A way of releasing your calendar in batches. Instead of keeping every date open, you open a set block of dates, such as the next four weeks, on an announced day and time. Until the next drop, dates beyond the current block cannot be booked.

### Are booking drops fair to regular clients?

Not by themselves, because a drop rewards whoever is online when it opens. They become fair when you book regulars at the chair before each drop, or give them a head start by message, so they never have to compete for their usual slot.

### How often should I do an appointment drop?

Often enough that each drop carries you to the next, which for a booked-out artist usually means monthly or every two weeks. The exact rhythm matters less than keeping it fixed so clients can plan around it.
