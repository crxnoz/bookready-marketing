The StyleSeat app does two jobs at once. It is the booking tool a stylist, barber or braider runs their calendar from, and it is a marketplace where clients search for someone nearby and book. You get both whether you want the second one or not, and the second one is where the fees that matter live.

So the useful question is not whether the app works. It is how much of your book will come from strangers searching, and how much from people who already know you.

:::answer
StyleSeat is a booking app and marketplace for hair and beauty pros in the US: you run your calendar, payments and no-show protection from it, and clients can find you by searching. You pay a monthly subscription, card processing on payments, and a fee on new clients the marketplace sends you, as a share of that first appointment. It suits a stylist who needs new clients; once your book is mostly regulars, check what you are paying for.
:::

## What the app does for you

On the pro side it covers the basics of running a book:

- An online booking page and a calendar, with your services, prices and hours.
- Card payments taken through StyleSeat's own processing.
- No-show and cancellation protection, which means the client's card is saved at booking under your policy.
- Reminders to clients before their appointment.
- A profile with your photos and reviews, shown inside StyleSeat's search.

Clients book through the StyleSeat site or app, with an account of their own. That is fine for them, and it also means the relationship runs through StyleSeat's front door, not yours.

## How the money works, in rough terms

There are three parts, and you should price all three, not just the first.

1. **The subscription.** A monthly fee to use the software, usually after a free trial.
2. **Card processing.** A percentage of each payment, plus a small fixed amount, taken before the money reaches you.
3. **The new client fee.** When the marketplace brings you a new client, StyleSeat takes a share of that first appointment. It has been sold as paying only for clients StyleSeat found for you.

Prices and fee rules change, and the third one has changed more than once. Do not trust a number from any article, this one included. Read StyleSeat's current terms on its own site before you sign up.

Here is a worked example, with round numbers that are not StyleSeat's: say a new client books a $150 sew-in through search, and the platform takes a fifth of it. That is $30 for the introduction. If that client comes back every six weeks for two years, it was a cheap introduction. If they never come back, it was the price of one appointment's profit.

> The new client fee is fair when the client really was new to you. Check how the platform decides that.

## Who it genuinely suits

StyleSeat is strong in the trades it grew up with: hair stylists, braiders, natural hair and loc specialists, barbers. For those pros it has real advantages.

- **You are building a book.** A new stylist, or one who just moved, gets found by people searching nearby.
- **Your clients already use it.** Plenty of clients look for a braider or stylist there first, and expect to book that way.
- **You want no-show protection without setting it up yourself.** The card hold comes with the app.

It suits you less if your chair is full of regulars who came from your Instagram, your old salon or word of mouth. Then you are paying for search you do not need, and every visit to your profile shows them other stylists too.

## What to check before you rely on it

Rely is the right word. Once your regulars have your StyleSeat link saved, moving them is work. So check these before that happens.

:::copy Questions to answer before you rely on the StyleSeat app

1. What is the monthly subscription today, from StyleSeat's own site?
2. What is the processing rate, and how long do payouts take to reach my bank?
3. Exactly which clients count as new for the new client fee? What if they found me on Instagram and then booked through my StyleSeat profile?
4. Is there a cap on the new client fee, and does it apply to the first visit only?
5. Can I turn off marketplace search and keep the booking tool, and what would that cost?
6. Can I export my client list at any time, and what is in the file?
7. What happens to my reviews, profile and link if I leave?

:::

The third question is the one that decides the cost for most busy stylists. If a client you sent there yourself can count as new, the fee is landing on clients you found.

## Do this week: sort your last thirty bookings

Open your calendar and sort your last thirty appointments into three groups:

1. Genuinely new clients who found you by searching inside StyleSeat.
2. Regulars, who would have found you anyway.
3. People you sent there yourself, from your bio link, a DM, a text or a card.

If group one is large, the marketplace is doing its job and its fees are paying for themselves. If it is one or two, you are paying for distribution you have stopped using, and the link in your bio could go somewhere you own.

## If you outgrow it

The honest trade-off with leaving any marketplace: nobody will find you from search inside an app any more. The finding becomes your job, through Instagram, Google, referrals and the card on the counter.

What you get in exchange is a booking page where you are the only stylist on it, at a link you control. BKRDY is one way to do that, a site of your own with booking, deposits and your policies, and it is not a marketplace. ## Questions

### Is the StyleSeat app free for stylists?

No. Clients use it for free, but stylists pay a monthly subscription after the trial, card processing on payments, and a fee on new clients the marketplace sends. Prices change, so check StyleSeat's own site for the current price.

### What is the StyleSeat new client fee?

A share of the first appointment with a client StyleSeat counts as new, brought to you through its marketplace. The rules for what counts as new, and any cap, have changed over time, so read the current terms before you rely on it.

### Who is StyleSeat best for?

Hair stylists, braiders and barbers in the US who need new clients finding them, especially when starting out or after a move. Once your book is mostly regulars, the marketplace fees are worth checking against where your clients really come from.
