Most cancellation waitlists are a list of names nobody uses. Someone cancels at nine in the morning, you scroll a note of twenty people, cannot remember who wanted what, message two of them, and by the time anyone replies the slot is gone.

A waitlist fills freed slots when it holds the right three facts about each person and you offer fast, to one person at a time, with a clock on it. That works with a note on your phone. It works better with something that does the offering for you.

:::answer
To run a cancellation waitlist that fills slots, put on it only people who want a time sooner than you can give them, and write down the days, times and service each one wants. When a slot frees up, offer it straight away to the first person it fits, give them a short time to claim it, then move to the next. Clear the list every month so it stays short enough to use.
:::

## Who goes on it

A waitlist is for people who want to come in sooner than your next real opening. Three kinds of client belong there:

- **The regular who asked for a full day.** They wanted Saturday, Saturday is full, and they would rather wait than move.
- **The new client you cannot fit for weeks.** They are keen now. In three weeks they may have booked somewhere else.
- **The client who booked later than they wanted.** They took the slot you had, and would take an earlier one if it came up.

That last group matters. Book every waitlist client into a real appointment as well, whenever you can. The waitlist is a way to come in sooner, not instead. If they claim an earlier slot, their later one frees up, and that one goes to the next person.

## What they tell you

Three facts, and you need all of them, because a name without them is useless at nine in the morning.

1. **The service.** A 30 minute brow wax cannot go into a slot freed by a two hour full set without leaving a gap, and the other way round does not fit at all.
2. **The days.** "Any weekday" or "Saturdays only".
3. **The times.** "After 4" or "mornings". And how much notice they need: someone who works shifts may only be able to take a slot with a day's warning.

Add the date they joined. You will need it.

## How fast to offer

As fast as you can. A slot that frees up today is the hardest to fill, and every hour you wait is an hour the client on your list has had to make other plans.

If you do it by hand, keep the list where you can reach it in the time it takes to read the cancellation. Not in a notebook at the studio. On your phone.

Do not send offers late at night. A slot offered at eleven is answered at seven, and by then you could have offered it twice.

## First come, or first fit

First fit, then first come. Go down the list in the order people joined, and offer the slot to the first person whose service, day and time match it. Someone at the top of the list who only wants Saturday mornings does not get a Tuesday at 2.

That is fair, and it is easy to explain: you offer openings in the order people asked, to whoever the opening suits.

## How long an offer stays open

Short, and stated. The closer the slot, the shorter the window.

- **Today or tomorrow:** an hour or two.
- **Later this week:** a few hours.
- **Next week:** half a day.

Say the deadline in the message itself. "Let me know by 1pm" gets an answer. "Let me know if you want it" gets one tomorrow.

When the time runs out, move on to the next person who fits, and do not apologise for it. They are still on the list for the next opening.

:::copy Message offering a freed slot
Hi Priya, a spot just opened for a brow wax tomorrow, Thursday, at 2pm. You are next on my waitlist, so it is yours if you want it. Let me know by 11am today and I will book you in. If it does not work, no problem, you stay on the list.
:::

Offer to one person at a time. If you send it to five, two will say yes, and one of them will be disappointed by someone they never met. If you do message several at once, say plainly that the first to reply gets it.

## What to do with a list of thirty names

A list that long has stopped working. Most of those people have booked elsewhere, moved on, or forgotten they asked. Offering a slot to them one at a time takes the whole morning.

Three habits keep it short:

- **Clear it monthly.** Once a month, send one message to everyone on it: still want an earlier time? Take off anyone who does not answer within a few days.
- **Give entries an end date.** A request for "an earlier fill in November" ends when November does.
- **Sort it by day.** Group the names under the days they want. When Thursday frees up, you read the Thursday names, not all thirty.

A long list is also a signal: you have more demand than hours, and it may be time to look at your prices.

## By hand, or let it run

A note on your phone with those three facts and a join date is enough to start. For a handful of cancellations a month it may be all you ever need.

The cost is your attention. Someone has to see the cancellation, read the list, write the message, watch the clock and move to the next name, often while you are working on a client.

## The trade-off

A waitlist will not fill every cancellation. A slot freed an hour before it starts is hard to fill however good the list, and some services are simply booked by people who plan weeks ahead.

It also takes some upkeep, by hand or not. Lists go stale, people forget they asked, and an offer nobody claims is a slot you might have filled by posting it yourself. Treat the waitlist as the first place a freed slot goes, not the only one.

## Questions

### How do I run a salon waitlist without software?

Keep a note on your phone with each person's name, service, days, times and the date they joined. When someone cancels, go down the list in order, offer the slot to the first person it fits, give them a deadline, and move to the next if they do not answer.

### Should I offer a cancelled slot to the first person on the list?

To the first person it fits. Go in the order people joined, but skip anyone whose service, days or times do not match the slot.

### How long should a waitlist offer stay open?

Shorter the closer the slot is. An hour or two for today or tomorrow, a few hours for later in the week, half a day for next week. Put the deadline in the message.
