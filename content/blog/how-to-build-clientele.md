A clientele is not built by strangers finding you. It is built by a few people who already know you coming back, and each of them sending one more. The first fifty clients usually come from your phone, not from an ad.

That makes the first year less about marketing and more about two habits: getting the next visit booked before anyone leaves, and asking for the referral out loud.

:::answer
Start with the people who already know you, and message them directly with one clear offer and one link that books. Rebook every client before they leave the chair, ask happy clients by name who they know, and post the work you want more of. A clientele grows from repeat visits and referrals far more than from new strangers.
:::

## Start with the people who already know you

Write down every person you could message today: friends, family, old coworkers, classmates, the people you see at the gym or at church. Most new stylists stop at ten names. Keep going to fifty.

Then message them one at a time, not in a group chat. A personal message gets read. A broadcast gets scrolled past.

:::copy First message to people you know
Hi Jordan, I just started taking clients on my own and I'm building my book. I'd love to do your hair. I have openings next week, and you can see my prices and times here: [your booking link]. If it's not for you, would you send it to one person who might want it?
:::

Ten messages a day for a week is fifty people. Some book, some forward it, some say nothing. That is normal, and it is still the fastest start there is.

## Make one link that books

Every message, post and business card should end in the same place: a page where someone picks a service, sees the price and books a time without messaging you first. If booking means a back and forth in your DMs, some people give up before you answer.

The page needs your services with prices and how long each takes, your open times, your policies, and your photos. BKRDY's Free plan gives you a booking page like that at yourname.bkrdy.me at no cost, which is enough to start. Whatever you use, put the link in your Instagram bio and keep it the same everywhere.

## Rebook before they leave the chair

The cheapest new appointment you will ever get is the next one from the client already sitting with you. Before they stand up, name the date.

"Your color will need a refresh in about six weeks. Want me to put you in for Thursday the 12th?" works better than "Text me when you need me." A specific date gets a yes or a different date. An open invitation gets forgotten.

The [guide to rebooking clients](/guides/how-to-rebook-clients/) has the scripts for the clients who hesitate.

## Ask for the referral, by name

"Tell your friends about me" rarely works, because it asks the client to think of someone. Do the thinking for them.

"Who do you know who's been wanting a new cut?" or "Does your sister still go to the place downtown?" gets a name. Then hand them something to send: your link, or a card with it printed on.

If you want to thank people for referrals, keep it simple and decide it before you start, such as something off their next visit once the person they sent comes in. Apply it the same way to everyone.

## Post the work you want more of

Your Instagram is your portfolio for people you have not met. Post the cuts and colors you want to be booked for, not everything you did that week. If you post twenty blowouts, you will get booked for blowouts.

Good light, a plain background and the same angle every time do more than editing. Say the service name in the caption so people know what to book. The [content ideas guide](/guides/content-ideas-for-beauty-businesses/) has a year of posts if you run dry.

## Be findable where people search

Plenty of people still search "hair salon near me" or "barber near me". A free Google Business Profile with your photos, hours and booking link puts you on the map for those searches.

Ask every client for a review, not just the ones you think loved it, and never offer anything in return for one. Reviews build slowly, which is why starting now matters.

## The trade-off

The quick way to fill a new book is a big first-visit discount. It works, and it often fills your chair with people who were shopping for the discount and do not come back at full price.

If you discount, keep it small and make the second visit easy to book at your real price. A slower start with clients who pay your price is a stronger book a year from now than a fast start you have to replace.

## Do this week

Write your list of fifty names. Send ten messages a day with your link. Rebook every client before they leave, and ask three of them by name who they know.

## Questions

### How long does it take to build a clientele as a hairstylist?

It depends on how many people you start with and how often your clients come back. Rebooking every client and asking for referrals speeds it up more than anything you post. Expect it to take months rather than weeks.

### How do I get clients as a new hairstylist with no following?

Start with the people who already know you. Message them one at a time with a clear offer and a link that books, then rebook each client before they leave and ask them by name who they know.

### Should I offer discounts to get new clients?

A small first-visit offer can help, but a big discount tends to attract people who leave when you charge full price. Make the second visit easy to book at your real price.
