The best moment to get a rebooking is while the client is still in the chair, looking at the result in the mirror. The second best is at the counter on the way out. A QR code for your booking page belongs in exactly those places, because that is where clients already stand with a phone in their hand.

It is a small thing. A printed square that saves a client from searching for your Instagram, finding the link in your bio, and giving up halfway.

:::answer
A booking QR code is a printed code that opens your booking page when a client points their phone camera at it. Put it where clients already stand: the station mirror, the checkout counter, the back of your business card, the receipt. Point it at your booking page itself, not your Instagram, test it on two different phones before you print, and remember it is only as good as the page behind it.
:::

## Put it where clients already stand

A QR code works when someone has a reason to scan it right then. Think about the moments in a visit when a client is still, has a phone out, and is thinking about next time.

- **The station mirror.** A small card in the corner of the mirror, at eye level when they are sitting. They are looking at the finished cut or the new braids, and the next appointment is on their mind.
- **The checkout counter.** Beside where they pay, or tip. The question "when should I come back?" usually comes up here.
- **The back of your business card.** A card goes home in a pocket. A code on it saves typing a link they will never type.
- **The receipt or aftercare card.** Anything a client takes with them.
- **The front window or door.** For people walking past when you are closed, who want to book without calling.

Skip the places nobody waits. A code on the wall behind your chair is a code nobody sees.

## Point it at your booking page, not your Instagram

The point of the code is one step from scanning to booking. If it opens your Instagram, the client still has to find the link in your bio, tap it, and then book. Every extra step loses some of them.

So the code should open the page where they choose a service and a time. That page should show your services with prices and durations, your open times, and your policies, and work on a phone without zooming.

Instagram is still worth having, and a code on a flyer can point there if the point is your portfolio. The code on your counter has one job.

> A QR code is a shortcut. Make sure it is a shortcut to the booking, not to the long way round.

## Make the code, then test it properly

You can make one in two ways.

**From your booking software.** Many booking tools offer a QR code for your page. BKRDY has one called "QR for your counter" on the Home page, which you download as an image for printing, and it opens your booking page at yourname.bkrdy.me.

**From any free QR generator.** Paste the link to your booking page and download the image. This works for any link. One caution: some generators make a code that sends people through their own service first, and a few stop working when a free trial ends. Choose one that puts your own link straight into the code, which some call a static code.

Then test it before you print a single card:

1. Scan it with an iPhone camera and with an Android phone, not only your own.
2. Scan it from where the client will be: sitting in the chair for the mirror, standing at the counter.
3. Scan it in the real light of your space, including the dim corner.
4. Check it opens the booking page, not a home page or a login screen.
5. Book a test appointment all the way through, then cancel it.

Print it big enough to scan from where people stand, with plain white space around it, dark on light. A code on a busy patterned background, or squeezed into a corner of a card, is the one that fails.

## Put a line of words beside it

A bare code asks people to trust it. A short line says what happens when they scan, and gives them a reason to.

:::copy Line to print under your QR code

Scan to book your next appointment. Prices, open times and policies are all on the page.

:::

:::copy Small card for the station mirror

Love it? Book your next one before you go.
Scan with your phone camera.

:::

Keep the wording short enough to read from the chair.

## The trade-off: a code is only as good as the page behind it

A QR code does not do anything on its own. It moves a client from the counter to a page faster. If the page is slow, confusing, or shows no open times, you have just sent them there quicker.

Three things break it most often.

- **The link changes.** You move booking platforms, or rename your page, and every printed code now opens an error. Before you print hundreds of cards, be sure the link will last. A link you control is the one to print.
- **Nothing is open.** The client scans, sees no times for six weeks, and closes it. If your books open in batches, say so on the page so an empty week reads as planned.
- **The page is not built for a phone.** Almost everyone scanning is on a phone. If your page needs pinching and zooming, fix that before you print anything.

If your booking page is a marketplace profile, the code works, but it lands the client in a listing beside other shops. For a code on your own mirror, a page that is only yours makes more sense, which is the case for [a booking page of your own](/blog/the-real-role-of-client-facing-booking-pages/).

## Do this week

Make the code, test it on two phones, and print one card for the station mirror and one for the counter. Leave them up for a month. Then check how many bookings came in while the client was still with you or that same evening. It is a cheap test, and a card costs almost nothing to move if the spot is wrong.

While you are at it, put the same link in your Instagram bio, so every route leads to the same page. The [guide to adding your booking link to Instagram](/guides/add-booking-link-to-instagram-bio/) covers that half.

## Questions

### What should a booking QR code link to?

Your booking page itself, where a client picks a service and a time. Not your Instagram, and not a home page they have to click through. One step from scanning to booking is the point.

### Where should I put a QR code for booking appointments?

Where clients already stand with a phone in hand: the station mirror, the checkout counter, the back of your business card, the receipt, and the front window for people passing when you are closed.

### Do free QR code generators work for booking pages?

Yes, for any link. Choose one that puts your own link straight into the code rather than routing people through its service, because some of those stop working when a free trial ends. Test it on two phones before you print.
