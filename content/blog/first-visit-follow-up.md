The cheapest thing you can do this week is message everybody who came in for the first time last week. One each, the day after, checking the work is sitting right and saying the one thing that keeps it looking good.

Almost nobody does it. The first visit gets all the effort, then silence, and the client decides on their own whether they are coming back to you or going back to where they used to go.

:::answer
Send one message the day after a client's first appointment, or two days after for anything that settles like lashes, nails or colour. It checks the work is sitting right, gives the one piece of aftercare that matters for that service, and says when the work will need attention again. Do not put a discount in it, do not ask for a follow, and do not chase a reply.
:::

## The first visit is the only one where silence costs you the client

A client of two years who hears nothing books again anyway. The habit carries them: they know they are due in three weeks and they know who they go to.

A first-timer has none of that. They are still deciding, comparing you with the place they left on things that have nothing to do with skill: whether they felt rushed, whether they knew what to do with the work once they got home. Most of that decision happens in the three or four days after they walk out. The rest of [client retention](/guides/client-retention-strategies/) is about people who come back already. This is about whether they start.

> A regular who hears nothing comes back anyway. A first-timer who hears nothing has learned something about you.

## Send it the day after, not the same evening

The same-hour message feels prompt and reads as automated. It also asks the client for an opinion on work they have not lived with. They have not slept on it, washed it or shown anybody, so there is nothing honest to say except thank you.

A week later fails the other way: the aftercare is useless and they have already decided. So the day after for a cut, a beard trim, a brow wax, a facial. Two days after for anything that settles, meaning lashes, a fresh set, colour, anything where the first 48 hours change how it looks. A Thursday 2pm fill gets a message on Saturday morning.

## What the message is for, and what has no business in it

Three jobs, in this order.

1. **Check the work is sitting right.** One specific question about the actual service, not "how did everything go?". Whether the colour settled where they wanted it, whether the fade grew in even.
2. **Give the one piece of aftercare that matters.** One. Not the five-point list you said out loud in the chair, because they were looking in the mirror and heard none of it.
3. **Leave the door open.** A plain statement of when the work will need attention. Not a question.

It is not for selling, and the moment it reads as a sales message it stops working. No discount, which turns aftercare into a promotion and teaches a new client to wait for the next one. No request to follow your Instagram, which is a favour you have not earned yet. No paragraph about your values. And nothing that obliges a reply out of politeness, so put "no need to reply" in it.

## Ask every client for a review, or do not ask at all

This is a good place for a review request, while the work is fresh. The rules are not optional, and breaking them can get a business's reviews taken down.

- **Ask every single client**, not only the ones who seemed pleased.
- **Never offer anything in return.** No discount, no free add-on, no entry into a draw.
- **Never write reviews yourself**, and never have staff, friends or family write them.
- **Never ask how they felt first and then send the link only to the happy ones.** This is the tempting one, because it looks like good judgement. Asking a satisfaction question and routing the positive answers to your review page while the unhappy ones go to a private form is review gating, and it is against Google's policies.

Ready wording is in [how to ask for reviews](/guides/ask-for-reviews-templates/). Asking everybody means the occasional three star, and that is the price of a review profile nobody can take away from you.

## Tell them when the work needs attention, not when you want them back

"Would you like to rebook?" is a question about your calendar, and the client has to work out the answer. The stronger version gives them the interval and the reason, and the reason is about their lashes, not your week.

"Fills hold best at about three weeks, so you are due the first week of December" does the thinking for them. So does "that fade will start losing its shape around week four". One sentence, no pressure. Rebooking as a habit belongs at the chair, and [rebooking clients](/guides/how-to-rebook-clients/) covers that.

## One line about them, and the rest the same every time

Keep one line that is genuinely about the client, from what you noted during the appointment, and let the rest be identical for everybody. That line is the difference between a message that lands and one they can tell went to forty people. It has to be specific: they sleep on their left side, they are growing out a fringe, they have a wedding in March. BKRDY keeps a client record with notes from each visit, so the line has somewhere to come from, and it can send the review request for you.

:::copy The first-visit follow up, two versions
For lashes, nails or colour, sent two days after:
Hi Dani, checking in now your lashes have had a couple of days to settle. Are they sitting right?
The one thing this week: brush them through dry every morning with the little wand, that is what keeps them looking full.
Your line to change: you said you sleep on your left side, so watch that outer corner and tell me if it loosens early.
Fills hold best at about three weeks, so you are due the first week of December. No need to reply.
If you have a minute, a review helps people find me more than anything else does. Link below.

For a cut, a beard trim or a brow wax, sent the day after:
Hi Marcus, how did the fade sit once you washed it?
The one thing this week: keep the back dry for a day if you can, it holds the line longer.
Your line to change: you said the last place always took it too high on the sides, so tell me if you want it lower next time.
It will start losing shape around week four, so mid December. No need to reply.
If you have a minute, a review helps people find me more than anything else does. Link below.
:::

Paste your own review link under the last line. Leave the rest alone.

## Most people will not reply, and that is the normal result

Expect silence. A few will say thank you, one or two will book, and the majority read it and do nothing. It still did its job.

One message, no chase. A second one asking why they did not answer turns a neutral into a negative, and it is the fastest way to make someone who was quietly happy feel managed.
