GlossGenius vs Booksy, and which one you need

November 13, 2026 · 6 min read

These two are not competing for the same job, which is why a feature list tells you almost nothing. Booksy is a marketplace: clients search for a beard trim near them and find you listed beside every other pro in your zip code. GlossGenius has no marketplace. It gives you a booking page and card payments in one subscription, and the people using it already know your name.

So the question is not which is better. It is whether you still need to be found by strangers, or need to serve the people who already found you.

The short answer

If you are still filling your book and nobody knows your name, Booksy earns its fee: it puts you in front of people already searching for your service. If your book runs on regulars, a marketplace is charging you to introduce you to clients you already have, and GlossGenius or your own site is the better buy. Count how many of your last thirty bookings were new people who found you inside the app. That number decides it.

They answer two different questions

Booksy sells attention. A stranger who has never heard of you can land on your profile, see your prices, and book Tuesday at 2pm without speaking to you first. For a barber who moved cities last month, that is worth real money.

GlossGenius sells tidiness. One subscription, one bill, a booking page that looks considered, card payments included, deposits that understand a 45 minute lash fill. Nobody discovers you through it. It assumes you already have clients. That is the whole comparison, and everything else hangs off it.

Run the last thirty bookings test

Go back through your last thirty bookings and put each one in a bucket:

  1. A regular, or someone a regular referred.
  2. Someone you sent to the link yourself: Instagram, a text, your Google listing, a card you handed them.
  3. A brand new person who found you by searching inside the app, with no push from you.

Only bucket three is what a marketplace is actually selling you. Eight or ten out of thirty, and the listing pays for itself. One or two, and everyone in those thirty would have booked you just as easily on a page of your own. Count again in six months, because the number moves.

Being listed where clients already browse is genuinely valuable while your book is not full. It is a cost you keep paying once it is.

How the money grows on each

Both change their prices, so price the tier you would actually be on from their own pages. What matters is the shape, because the shape is what bites you in year two. Booksy tends to charge for each extra staff member on top of the base subscription, plus card processing, plus an optional paid promotion that lifts you in marketplace search. GlossGenius tends toward a flatter subscription with payments built in at its own card rates. The marketplace side is broken down further in the Booksy pricing article.

The promotion is the part people misread. It is not a one-off boost. As more competitors pay to rank, staying visible costs more rather than less, so the spend that got you seen last spring buys less this spring. Budget it as a standing marketing line, or do not start it.

Neither page is really yours

On Booksy, clients book on a Booksy profile, in Booksy's layout, with other local pros a scroll away. On GlossGenius you get your own booking page, in their style, on their platform.

GlossGenius is clearly the better of the two if looking like everyone else is what bothers you. But be honest about what that buys: a nicer house style, not a house. Neither page is a site you own outright, and on both the structure is decided for everybody on the platform. Fixing that means a booking site with a design of its own, a separate decision from this one, and where BKRDY sits.

Who should pick which

New in a dense city, nobody knows your name. Booksy. You need introductions more than a tidy page, and introductions are what you are buying.

An established book of regulars, tired of paying for introductions to your own clients. GlossGenius, or your own site. The marketplace has stopped doing the one thing it is good at, and the fee has turned into rent.

Somewhere in between, which is most people. Stay on the marketplace and build somewhere of your own at the same time. Keep the listing earning while it earns, send every regular and referral to your own link, and watch bucket three shrink until the listing is optional. Slower than switching in a weekend, and the honest answer nobody gives you. The wider field is in this comparison of booking platforms.

Keep an export, then send one message

Export your client list every few months on any platform, including your own, and check the names, numbers and emails came through. Ordinary housekeeping, like backing up photos, not a dig at either company.

Those contacts are what comes with you. What does not: the booking link saved in your clients' phones, the reviews on that platform's profile, and the page itself. Which is why moving regulars is a conversation, not an export.

Message to send clients when you move them to your own link

Hi Jess, quick one so you don't get stuck next time you book. My booking has moved to my own page: yourname.com/book It's faster for you. No app, no account to set up, and you'll see my full menu with add-ons and real prices instead of messaging me to ask. Twenty seconds to grab your usual Saturday slot. Save the link now if you can. The old one stops working on the 1st and I'd hate for you to lose your spot.

Send it a week before the old link dies, then again two days before. Change your Instagram bio link and your Google Business Profile booking link the same day. The Google one is what people forget, and it quietly sends new clients to a dead page for months.

Questions

Is GlossGenius better than Booksy?

For an established pro with a book of regulars, usually yes, because you stop paying a marketplace to introduce you to clients you already have. For someone new in a busy city with no word of mouth, Booksy is better: being listed where clients already search beats a tidier page.

Which one is cheaper?

It depends on your staff count and whether you buy promotion. Booksy tends to add cost per extra staff member plus processing plus promotion, and GlossGenius tends toward a flatter subscription with payments built in at its own card rates. Both change their prices, so check their own pages.

What happens to my reviews if I leave either one?

They stay on that platform's profile, which is a good reason to be collecting reviews on your Google Business Profile too. Ask every client rather than only the happy ones, never offer anything in exchange, never write or buy reviews, and never ask the rating question first so you can route unhappy people elsewhere. Those practices can get a business's reviews removed.
