Top 6 BookingBee.ai Software Alternatives 2026

Explore these 6 BookingBee.ai alternatives to find the best salon booking software that meets your needs for improved customer engagement.

Finding salon booking software that fits studio branding, payment handling, and daily client management without extra work remains a hassle for many beauty professionals. Many tools force you to split booking from your website, hide useful integrations behind upgrades, or bump up fees when your bookings grow. This list compares price, workflow support, and payment features across top salon booking tools so you can match one to your salon’s daily needs without extra tool setup.

BKRDY

At a Glance

BKRDY starts at $16/month billed annually and advertises no transaction fees and free migration from GlossGenius, Acuity, or Fresha. The platform packages a branded website with booking tools so solo beauty pros can replace generic booking links with a studio site. That setup reduces the need for separate hosting and a booking widget.

Core Features

BKRDY provides customizable website templates with gallery, policies, and banner content that match studio branding. The booking engine handles online scheduling with deposits, automatic reminders, an auto waitlist and cancellation flows, and two-way Google Calendar sync. The platform includes a branded client management hub with client history, multi-staff calendars, and review collection.

Key Differentiator

BKRDY’s standout feature is a fully designed, customizable website built specifically for beauty professionals combined with built-in booking and payment handling. The package removes the common split between a marketing site and a scheduling app by hosting both under the studio’s brand and domain.

Pros

BKRDY gives beauty studios a visually focused site and booking experience that reflects their brand. The templates support galleries and service pages so portfolios and policies look intentional rather than tacked on. Payment handling uses Stripe, which lets studios collect deposits and avoid in-platform transaction fees claimed by the vendor. Built-in client history and automated reminders reduce no-shows and keep notes on client preferences. Free migration support from common platforms shortens the setup window for studios moving from GlossGenius, Acuity, or Fresha.

Cons

  • Primarily US-focused with Stripe US payments and booking flow, which limits international card processing and regional payment options.

Who It’s For

BKRDY fits solo lash artists, nail technicians, and small hairstylists who want a branded website plus scheduling without stitching multiple tools together. It also suits small studios with one to a few service providers who need client notes and deposit flows tied to their site.

Unique Value Proposition

The most concrete advantage is that BKRDY combines a studio-grade website and booking engine in one product at a low entry price. That arrangement cuts the time you spend integrating a separate booking widget into a site and reduces monthly service fragmentation for studios that want brand ownership without high setup costs.

Real World Use Case

A solo lash artist switches from a marketplace booking link to BKRDY, imports client data during the free migration, sets deposit rules for high-value treatments, and publishes a gallery-driven site. The artist then uses client history and reminders to tailor follow-ups and reduce last-minute cancellations.

Pricing

BKRDY lists a founding member price starting at $16/month billed annually ($192/yr, saving $36 against monthly). A free plan with basic features is available and does not require a credit card to start.

Website: https://bkrdy.com

Heygoldie

At a Glance

Heygoldie reports serving over 150,000 beauty professionals worldwide. That claim appears alongside mobile and web apps that combine scheduling, payments, and AI tools. Public review threads praise the feature set but show repeated complaints about service reliability and support quality.

Core Features

The platform bundles appointment scheduling, automated reminders, a customizable booking website, and team management into mobile and web apps. It supports payments with a card reader, in-app product sales, inventory tracking, and integrations like Reserve with Google. Heygoldie also offers AI tools such as AI Marketer and an AI receptionist for messaging and campaign drafts.

Key Differentiator

The standout is the mix of salon-focused commerce and AI assistance. Heygoldie pairs client-facing booking and a booking website with merchant features like Buy Now, Pay Later options. That combination aims to keep service bookings, retail sales, and marketing inside one account.

Pros

The feature set matches common salon needs: booking, client records, reminders, and inventory control in one place. Mobile apps plus a web app make on-the-go booking and front-desk work possible. Integrations such as Reserve with Google and the vendor’s BNPL option add channels for bookings and retail sales. That figure about user count suggests the product has broad market adoption among beauty professionals.

Cons

  • Public review platforms show frequent reports of poor service experiences and unprofessional staff conduct. These reports cite inconsistent support and long resolution times.
  • Customers report reliability and transparency issues, including billing confusion and unclear refund processes. These complaints appear repeatedly in public feedback.
  • Allegations include missed appointments or inconsistent merchant handling, which could disrupt client trust and local operations.

When It May Not Fit

Businesses that require a high level of dependable customer support may want a different vendor. Salons that cannot tolerate billing or refund uncertainty should look elsewhere. Shops that depend on spotless public reputation and fast issue resolution might find the reported service problems unacceptable.

Who It’s For

Solo stylists and small salon owners who want a single app for bookings, payments, and basic marketing will find the feature set relevant. Owners who already sell products at the chair and want BNPL and inventory in one place will benefit. Larger multi-location salons that demand tight support SLAs should evaluate support quality carefully.

Real World Use Case

A neighborhood salon uses Heygoldie to automate online booking and send automatic reminders. The receptionist processes payments with the card reader and flags retail inventory when stock runs low. The owner uses AI Marketer to draft an email campaign and track responses through the web app.

Pricing

Heygoldie offers a free Starter plan and two paid tiers. Pro starts at $19.99/mo and Pro Plus at $39.99/mo for additional features and expanded tools. The pricing model centers on fixed monthly tiers rather than per-booking fees.

Website: https://heygoldie.com

salonist

At a Glance

Salonist reports being trusted by 15,000 salons worldwide. The vendor highlights a free tier and mobile apps for staff and customers to book on the go. The product markets itself as a full salon operations suite that covers bookings, POS, inventory, and marketing.

Core Features

Salonist combines customizable booking and appointment management with customer profiles that store service history and notes. The platform also includes a point of sale system, inventory tracking, gift card and loyalty tools, and a form builder for consent and feedback. Multi-location support, marketing automation by email and SMS, and payment connectors such as Stripe, PayPal, and Razorpay round out the core feature set.

Key Differentiator

Salonist positions itself as an all-in-one operations platform rather than a single-purpose booking page. The product pairs scheduling with payroll, inventory, and marketing tools, which suits salons that want one system for back office and front-desk tasks. The presence of an extensive resource library also helps owners who prefer vendor-led templates and guides.

Pros

The interface is generally user friendly and focuses on daily salon workflows like multi-stylist scheduling and automated reminders for clients. Built-in POS and inventory reduce the need for a separate retail system, and the marketing features let you run campaigns from the same dashboard. Flexible plans and a free tier lower the barrier to trial, while multi-language support and wide third-party links help chains and international studios.

Cons

  • Reports of unresponsive customer service, which can delay technical or billing resolutions.

  • Allegations of billing and refund delays that have affected customer trust.

  • Occasional stability concerns and reported user blocking during disputes, which may interrupt day to day operations.

  • Advanced features and multi-location setup can feel complex without dedicated onboarding or paid support.

When It May Not Fit

If you need a clean, brand-first booking website rather than full operations software, Salonist may feel heavyweight. If rapid, hands-on vendor support matters, reported responsiveness issues could be a deal breaker. Single-operator studios that only want a minimal booking page might prefer a simpler, design-led tool.

Who It’s For

Small to medium beauty and wellness salons that need integrated management tools will get the most value. Multi-staff salons and short chains benefit from multi-location scheduling and inventory control. Owners planning to run marketing campaigns and sell products online will find the bundled features useful.

Real World Use Case

A chain of hair salons uses Salonist to manage appointments, staff rosters, inventory, and marketing across several locations. The company centralizes payroll and product sales in the same system to reduce reconciliation work. That setup frees managers to focus on service quality and retention.

Pricing

Salonist offers a free tier and paid plans starting at $79/month when billed monthly and at $799 billed annually, with higher tiers adding inventory, marketing campaigns, and multi-location tools. Exact feature limits by tier are listed on the vendor site.

Teamtailor

At a Glance

Some Teamtailor accounts lack two factor authentication, and users report time consuming data migration when moving legacy records. The platform pairs a clear, user friendly interface with candidate tracking and branded job pages. Customer support and frequent feature updates receive positive mention from customers.

Core Features

Teamtailor centers on candidate management workflows that let hiring teams track applicants, move candidates through stages, and centralize communication. The product includes employer branding tools for custom careers pages and job page design that keeps role listings on brand. Regular product updates and an emphasis on easy use round out the core feature set.

Key Differentiator

Teamtailor stands out for focusing on employer branding alongside applicant tracking. Its combination of a clear, user friendly interface and design led job pages helps companies present roles like marketing assets. The vendor highlights active customer support and frequent updates as part of that brand focused approach.

Pros

The interface is straightforward, which shortens the learning curve for HR staff and hiring managers. Employer branding tools let teams publish careers pages that match studio or salon aesthetics without extra design work. Support responsiveness and visible product updates keep small recruiting teams confident that issues get attention.

Cons

  • Time consuming data migration. Migrating large or complex candidate histories can take prolonged effort and manual cleanup.
  • Partial lack of security features. Some accounts do not have two factor authentication available.
  • No external client access for job postings. Agencies or studios that want clients to review listings cannot add external clients to postings.

When It May Not Fit

If you need strict access controls and universal two factor authentication for every account, Teamtailor may fall short. Businesses with large legacy databases should plan for a slow migration and possible manual steps. If you require client level access to job listings, the platform does not support adding external clients to postings.

Who It’s For

HR teams and recruiters at mid sized to large companies who want an applicant tracking system that prioritizes brand presentation will find a close fit. Small recruiting teams that value a low learning curve and responsive support will also benefit. It suits teams that can tolerate some migration friction and selective security gaps.

Real World Use Case

A mid sized company uses Teamtailor to run job campaigns and publish a careers site that mirrors studio branding. Recruiting coordinators manage applicant stages, send messages, and tag candidates for follow up. The company relies on support and feature updates to refine workflows while addressing migration work over time.

Pricing

Pricing is listed as informational only and no public tiered pricing is provided. Prospective buyers should contact Teamtailor sales for a custom quote and to confirm which security features are included for their account.

Website: https://timetailor.com

IdleBooking

At a Glance

IdleBooking packages online booking, POS, inventory, staff scheduling, and AI client tools under a single flat subscription rather than charging per user or per booking. The vendor positions the product for beauty and wellness businesses from solo operators to multi-location chains. The platform is Singapore based and includes automated WhatsApp receptionist features and Stripe payment support.

Core Features

The calendar and booking system handles staff assignments, deposits, branded booking links, and a smart waitlist that manages cancellations and fills open slots. IdleBooking includes a point of sale with Stripe payments, inventory tracking with expiry dates and AI restock suggestions, and package and margin tracking with GST handling. The product also bundles an AI client CRM, automated WhatsApp communications, role based access control, multi-store support, and analytics in a single interface.

Key Differentiator

IdleBooking’s main selling point is that it keeps every core tool behind one flat subscription with built-in automation for beauty and wellness workflows. That approach removes per-user or per-booking fees from the pricing equation while centralizing booking, POS, inventory, and client communications. The automation focus includes client briefings and a WhatsApp AI receptionist tuned for salon and spa inquiries.

Pros

The platform aims to reduce the number of separate tools you run by combining scheduling, payments, and inventory into one product, which simplifies bookkeeping and staff coordination. IdleBooking advertises no booking commissions or per-user fees and instead relies on a flat subscription model, which can make monthly costs more predictable. Its WhatsApp automation and AI client briefings reduce the manual booking and intake work that often eats time in busy salons.

Cons

  • No third-party user reviews are available, which makes it hard to verify real world reliability or support expectations.
  • Pricing details are provided only after contacting sales or requesting a demo, so cost comparisons require direct engagement.
  • Integration details outside of Stripe and WhatsApp are limited, which may complicate connections to niche local tools or regional payment rails.

When It May Not Fit

If your studio relies on public user feedback to judge stability or support, IdleBooking’s lack of third-party reviews will feel risky. If you need transparent published pricing for quick vendor comparison, the sales contact requirement slows procurement. If your workflow depends on many specific third-party integrations, the limited integration list may not cover everything you use.

Notable Integrations

  • Stripe for payments and payout handling.

Who It’s For

This product fits beauty and wellness owners who want one system for bookings, payments, inventory, and staff management rather than several specialized apps. It works for solo practitioners who want automation and for growing studios planning multiple locations. Owners who accept WhatsApp bookings and need role based access control for teams will find this relevant.

Real World Use Case

A mid sized hair salon chain uses IdleBooking to consolidate online reservations, staff schedules, and inventory across three locations. The salon moved routine reminders and client briefings to the AI tools and reduced front desk workload. That freed managers to focus on upsells and on-floor service quality.

Pricing

IdleBooking markets a flat subscription with no per-booking or per-user fees, but specific rate tiers are not published. You must request a demo or contact sales to get pricing and contract details.

Website: https://idlebooking.com

Anna

At a Glance

Anna includes a built-in discount marketplace that helps fill empty appointment slots. The app also supports booking via social media links and automatic client reminders. That mix targets self-employed beauty pros who need both bookings and quick ways to convert free time into revenue.

Core Features

Anna centralizes appointment management and automatic reminders while tracking bookings and basic financial analytics. It accepts bookings from social profiles and offers a marketplace where you can post discounts to fill free slots. The platform reports transparent pricing with no contracts or hidden fees.

Key Differentiator

Anna groups scheduling, reminders, analytics, and a discount marketplace into one simple SaaS offering for beauty professionals. That combination makes it easier to promote last-minute availability directly to potential clients. The product positions itself for small teams and solo operators rather than enterprise deployments.

Pros

Anna automates routine booking tasks and sends reminders to reduce no-shows, which saves time in daily operations. The analytics provide straightforward insights into booking trends and revenue so you can adjust hours or promotions. The marketplace gives a practical way to monetize slow periods, and pricing is clear with no long-term commitment.

Cons

  • Independent review coverage is limited, so external validation and detailed user stories are sparse.
  • The platform lacks public information about advanced enterprise features for larger salon groups.
  • Integrations with complex POS or enterprise resource planning systems are not listed.

When It May Not Fit

Anna may not match the needs of multi-location salons that require advanced staff payroll, inventory, or ERP features. Larger operations needing deep POS integration or multi-branch reporting will likely find the feature set narrow. Companies with complex scheduling rules or multi-service dependency chains should evaluate other tools.

Who It’s For

Anna suits self-employed beauty professionals, independent lash artists, nail technicians, and small salon owners. It fits owners who publish services on social profiles and rely on quick booking links to capture clients. Choose Anna if you want simple booking management plus a tool to convert idle time into bookings.

Real World Use Case

A nail technician uses Anna to accept bookings from Instagram, send automatic reminders, and track which days fill fastest. When gaps appear, she posts discounted slots in the marketplace and fills appointments the same week. That routine reduces empty hours and clarifies which services attract repeat bookings.

Pricing

Anna offers a LITE plan at $15.99/month and a PROFI plan at $21.99/month. The vendor advertises no contracts and no extra fees, which makes month-to-month testing low risk. Compare features between the two tiers before committing.

Website: https://getanna.app
