GlossGenius vs Square Appointments, honestly compared

October 26, 2026 · 6 min read

The honest comparison here is not a feature list, because these are not the same kind of product. One is built for beauty and sells you a bundle. The other is a booking calendar bolted onto a payments company that also serves coffee shops and dog groomers.

So the real question is what else your business sells, and how much you care what your booking page looks like.

The short answer

If you already run a register, retail or more than one business through Square, stay on Square Appointments. If you are a solo beauty pro who wants one subscription covering a booking page and card payments, and you like how their pages look, GlossGenius fits better. With both, the processing is theirs and the page is their design. That is the trade either way.

They are not the same kind of product

GlossGenius is beauty-native. The language in the app is your language: services, add-ons, deposits, tips, color notes, lash fills. It bundles a booking page and card processing into one subscription, so there is one bill. Card readers are sold separately. The design is deliberate and consistent, which is why solo lash artists, nail techs and stylists like it.

Square Appointments is a booking calendar sitting on Square's payments business. That is not a criticism, it is the point. Square's strength is the till, the inventory, the receipts, and running several revenue lines through one account.

The tell: GlossGenius understands a 45 minute lash fill with a $25 deposit. Square understands selling that fill plus a cleanser and a gift card in one checkout, then seeing all three in one end-of-day report.

Both own your card processing, which is the actual lock-in

With both platforms, the software and the payment processing are the same company. You do not bring your own processor. You do not shop a rate.

That matters three ways. Your true cost is the subscription plus a percentage of everything you take, so your bill grows with your revenue. Your payout timing, and any hold on your money, is set by the company that runs your calendar. And leaving means changing both on the same day.

Software you can switch in an afternoon. A merchant account holding your clients' saved cards is a different kind of move.

On price, both sit where a normal small-business subscription sits, tens of dollars a month rather than hundreds, with higher tiers for more staff. Square has long been the cheaper way in for one person. No figures belong here, because both change prices and change what each tier includes. Check each company's own page, find the tier you would be on, and add the processing percentage.

Stay on Square if your register is already Square

If a real share of your money comes through a till, stop comparing. An esthetician selling retail skincare, a barbershop with a product wall, a salon running gift cards: all are better off booking inside the account that already holds the inventory and the sales history.

Moving booking off Square while leaving payments there leaves two systems that each know half of a client's story, and you close that gap by hand every month.

The reverse holds too. No till, no retail, no second business, and you pay for the half of Square you never open.

Pick GlossGenius if you are solo and the look fits

For one person doing beauty work, GlossGenius is a sound, simple choice. One subscription, one support line, deposits and tips that work the way beauty appointments do.

Here is the honest limit. The page clients land on is a GlossGenius page in a GlossGenius layout. You get your logo, photos, colors and service list, not the structure or the overall feel.

That trade exists either way. Square decides because design is not its business. GlossGenius decides because a house style is part of what it sells. If your brand is part of the service, the third category is a booking site with a design of its own, which is where BKRDY sits. If your brand is your hands and your work, ignore all of this.

What moves with you, and what does not

Know the exit before you commit. It looks the same on both.

What you can take: client names, phone numbers and emails. Export them to a CSV now and every few months after, and keep the file somewhere that is yours. Do that whatever you decide.

What you cannot take: appointment history and notes come out messy or not at all, saved cards stay with the processor because the tokens are theirs, and your booking link breaks. If your plan lets you point your own domain at the booking page, set that up first. A link on your own domain is the one piece you can redirect later.

That link is in your Instagram bio, your Google listing, your text signature and a hundred old confirmation emails. When it changes you have to tell everyone, which is survivable if you say it well.

Message to send your client list when you move booking platforms

Quick note: my booking link has changed. New link: yoursite.com/book Same services, same prices, same chair. If you already have an appointment with me, it is still in my calendar and nothing changes for you. The old link stops working on November 1, so save this one. One small thing: if your card was saved on the old system, it did not come across. You will add it once at checkout and then it is saved again. Thank you for following me over. See you soon.

Send that twice, a week apart, and change your Instagram bio link the same day. Then update the booking link on your Google Business Profile, the one that quietly sends people to a dead page for months.

The decision is smaller than it feels. Square if the register is already yours, GlossGenius if you are solo in beauty and the style suits you. For the wider field, there is a broader comparison of salon booking software.

Questions

Is GlossGenius better than Square Appointments?

For a solo beauty professional with no retail and no register, usually yes, because it is built for beauty work and bundles the booking page and processing into one subscription. For a business already running a till, inventory or several revenue lines through Square, Square Appointments is the better fit.

Can I use my own payment processor with either one?

No. Both platforms process your card payments themselves, so the software and the merchant account are the same company. Your real cost is the subscription plus a percentage of everything you take, and leaving means changing both at once.

What happens to my client list if I leave GlossGenius or Square?

You can export client names, phone numbers and emails from both. Appointment history and notes often come out incomplete, saved cards do not transfer because the card tokens belong to the processor, and your booking link stops working unless it was on a domain you own.
