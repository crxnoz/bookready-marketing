Rosy Salon Software

At a Glance

Founded by former salon owners, Rosy focuses on workflows that match how salons actually operate. It pairs in-salon scheduling with online booking and messaging. The product starts from $44/month and includes a 14-day free trial for new accounts.

Core Features

Rosy combines appointment scheduling with automated reminders and online booking so clients can self-schedule. It sends text and email confirmations, offers mobile access for on-the-go management, and tracks stock with low stock alerts and order management. The system also includes point of sale and basic business reporting for daily operations.

Key Differentiator

Rosy was built by people who ran salons. That background shows in menu labels, booking flows, and default settings tuned to service times and retail behavior. The emphasis on ease of use and salon language makes initial setup faster for owners and booth renters.

Pros

The interface reads like it was made by salon staff. That reduces training time and helps owners adopt the calendar and POS quickly. Cloud based access and mobile control let you manage appointments and inventory away from the chair. Integration with RosyPay keeps payments inside the system so reconciliation stays straightforward. The price point and the included trial lower the barrier for small businesses to test the software.

Cons

  • Customization for complex enterprise needs appears limited compared with larger platforms.
  • Setting up multiple locations can require extra configuration and vendor support.
  • Marketplace and analytics capabilities may be less feature rich than some enterprise alternatives.

When It May Not Fit

If you run a large salon group you will likely need deeper customization and centralized controls than Rosy offers. If you require advanced multi location reporting or bespoke workflows you may hit limits. Larger operations that need heavy analytics will find the platform basic for those needs.

Notable Integrations

Rosy connects to RosyPay for payment processing, which keeps card transactions inside the same environment. The product listing does not show other third party integrations publicly.

Who It’s For

Small to medium salon and spa owners, independent booth renters, and solo stylists who want a single tool for booking, POS, and inventory work best with Rosy. It fits teams that value simplicity and industry-specific defaults. Owners who prefer minimal setup time will appreciate the salon-first design.

Real World Use Case

A boutique salon owner runs daily bookings from a tablet. She uses Rosy to confirm appointments, send reminders by text, and process payments through RosyPay at checkout. Inventory alerts trigger reorders so retail shelves stay stocked without manual counts.

Pricing

The vendor lists pricing starting at $44/month and offers a 14 day free trial for new customers. Tiers scale by number of employees, which affects seat counts and feature availability listed on the vendor site.

Website: https://rosysalonsoftware.com

Key Differentiator

Salonist’s main edge is its industry focus plus a broad set of built-in tools for both front desk and retail operations. A regional salon chain uses Salonist to publish online booking pages for each location and to keep a shared client database. At a Glance

Core Features

The core calendar handles appointment scheduling and staff assignments while keeping client histories and notes together. Configurable services let you set durations, prices, and deposit requirements for each offering. Consultation forms collect intake information before appointments so your team has context at booking.

Key Differentiator

Book in Beautiful focuses on giving salons control over their brand and revenue by eliminating commission on bookings. The platform emphasizes a brand-led booking presence with optional website and app integration tailored to UK salons. That positioning narrows the product to owners who prefer their own storefront over marketplace listings.

Pros

You keep booking revenue because the product charges no commission on appointments. Built-in client management stores histories and consultation answers, which reduces back-and-forth and improves service consistency. The platform supports staff scheduling for multi-stylist salons and handles deposits and cancellations so you can protect revenue and reduce no-shows. Easy migration from other systems is listed, which makes switching less disruptive for established salons.

Cons

  • Pricing and plans are customized, which may complicate budgeting for small businesses.
  • The vendor supplies limited detail on specific industry compliance features such as licensing or health standards.
  • The feature set may be more complex than needed for solo practitioners who require only basic scheduling.

Who It’s For

UK-based beauty salons and solo practitioners who want a branded booking presence with no marketplace commission will find this product a close fit. Multi-stylist salons that need staff management and deposit workflows benefit most. Owners who plan to run a custom website or branded app alongside bookings will get the most value.

Real World Use Case

A small salon replaces phone-based bookings with the platform and moves deposit collection online. Reception spends less time confirming appointments because consultation forms arrive before visits. Staff schedules update automatically, which reduces double bookings and frees time for service delivery.

Pricing

Pricing is customizable and depends on selected features and the number of locations or users. The vendor uses a calculator or provides a personalized quote rather than fixed public tiers. That model gives flexibility but requires a sales conversation to lock in monthly costs.
