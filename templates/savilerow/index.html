Savile Row

The name. The London street that made bespoke tailoring a craft. Measured, traditional, finished by hand.

Bespoke · Tailored · Old-world

A dark, warm-neutral canvas with brass accents and Zilla Slab display type, set like an engraved plaque. The hero is a type-left, portrait-right split with a tailor's spec-sheet frame around the photo: double hairline, corner registration ticks. Built for barbers, men's grooming studios, and traditional shops that want to read bespoke rather than streetwear.

Built for the traditional shop

A barbershop that reads bespoke, not streetwear.

A slab serif rather than another grotesque sans, a plate frame around the photography, and brass instead of neon. It sits deliberately apart from Blackline, which runs industrial and modern on the same dark ground. Same trade, opposite tailoring.

Already in the template

Every section a shop needs

Type-left hero with a framed portrait. Service menu with cuts, beard work, and add-ons. Barber bios with their own calendars. Gallery, hours, policies, and a booking flow with deposits and no-show protection. Loyalty punch cards and gift cards ride along on every template.

Who tends to pick it

Traditional barbers and men's grooming

Old-world and heritage barbershops. Men's grooming studios running hot towel and straight razor. Shops with a chair rental model and several barbers. If your brand is sharper and more industrial, Blackline is the closer fit. If it is streetwear-led, look at The Fade Room.
